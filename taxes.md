This guide serves three readers at once, and each section is layered so you can stop at the depth you need:

### The 30-second mental model

A **tax** in Odoo is one reusable rule (`account.tax`) that does two jobs: it turns a line amount into a tax amount, and it decides where that money lands in the ledger and on the legal tax return. The same rule is used everywhere — quotations, sales orders, invoices, purchase orders, vendor bills, POS receipts — so a "20% VAT" behaves identically no matter where you meet it. What changes per document is only *which* tax is selected: the product proposes a default, the company sets fallbacks, and the customer's **fiscal position** can swap it (e.g. domestic VAT → 0% for an EU export). One computation **engine** then produces the numbers, **repartition lines** route them to accounts and tag them for the tax report, and at period end a **closing entry** moves the net VAT to the tax authority.

> **Community vs Enterprise:** the engine, taxes, fiscal positions, repartition and grids all live in community `account`. The periodic **tax report** and the automatic **tax closing entry** are generated by the **Enterprise** `account_reports` module. Without Enterprise you keep the accounts and the lock date but must journal the VAT transfer by hand. See [Tax Report & Tax Closing Entry](#tax-report--tax-closing-entry).

## How Taxes Work in Odoo (Overview)

In Odoo, a "tax" is a configurable rule that turns an amount into a tax amount and decides where that money lands in accounting and on legal reports. The central object is `account.tax`, defined in [account_tax.py:56](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L56). This one model is reused everywhere — it sits on products, gets pulled onto sales orders, purchase orders, invoices, and POS orders, and is evaluated by a single computation engine (`compute_all` / the base-line methods). The `account` module owns the engine, the tax object, fiscal positions, repartition, and grids, and *stores* the tax-closing accounts on each tax group — but the periodic tax report and the posted closing entry are generated by the **enterprise** `account_reports` module (see [Tax Report & Tax Closing Entry](#tax-report--tax-closing-entry)). The `sale`, `purchase`, and `product` layers only *select* taxes and feed them into the engine; and the `l10n_*` localization modules ship country-specific taxes, grids, reports, and special regimes (withholding, cash basis). Because there is one engine, a 18% VAT behaves identically whether you hit it on a quotation, a vendor bill, or a shop receipt — only the *selection* of which tax applies changes by document and partner.

### Big Picture: end-to-end flow

```
 Company defaults                Product defaults
 account_sale_tax_id             taxes_id (sales)
 account_purchase_tax_id         supplier_taxes_id (purchase)
        |                               |
        +---------------+---------------+
                        v
            Order / Invoice line picks taxes
            (_compute_tax_ids -> _get_computed_taxes)
                        |
                        v
            Fiscal Position remaps them
            (fiscal_position_id.map_tax)
                        |
                        v
            Line.tax_ids  (the taxes actually applied)
                        |
                        v
        TAX ENGINE  (account.tax)
        compute_all / _add_tax_details_in_base_lines
        - price_include? (or the document's document_tax_mode) amount_type? sequence?
        - include_base_amount cascades to next tax
                        |
                        v
        Repartition lines split base & tax amounts
        (invoice_repartition_line_ids / refund_...)
        - factor_percent -> account_id, tag_ids
                        |
                        v
        Journal items + Tax grids (account.account.tag)
                        |
                        v
        Tax Report  --->  Tax Closing Entry   (enterprise: account_reports)
        community account owns only the grids; account_reports holds the
        payable/receivable accounts, computes the report and posts the closing move
```

The flow reads top to bottom. **Defaults** seed a starting tax: the company holds `account_sale_tax_id` / `account_purchase_tax_id` ([company.py:129](../addons/account/models/company.py#L129)), and a product carries `taxes_id` for selling and `supplier_taxes_id` for buying ([product.py:79](../addons/account/models/product.py#L79)). **Document lines** then choose taxes: an invoice line recomputes `tax_ids` from the product or the account in `_get_computed_taxes` ([account_move_line.py:1302](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1302)). **Fiscal position** is the remapping step — if the partner/document has one, `map_tax` swaps domestic taxes for their mapped equivalents ([partner.py:161](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L161); applied at [account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336)). The resulting `tax_ids` is fed to the **batch tax engine**: documents prepare a list of *base lines* and call `_add_tax_details_in_base_lines` ([account_tax.py:1798](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1798)), which produces base and tax amounts. (The old single-line `compute_all` at [account_tax.py:4848](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4848) still exists, but it is a thin backward-compatibility *shim* — it builds one base line, calls the same engine, and reshapes the result into the legacy dict; `account.move` and POS posting never call it. See [Tax Computation Methods](#tax-computation-methods-percent-fixed-group-division-python).) **Repartition lines** then distribute those amounts to GL accounts and attach **tax grids** (tags) for reporting. Finally the **tax report** aggregates by grid, and the **closing entry** moves the net VAT to the payable/receivable account configured on the enterprise `account.return.type` (**changed in 20** — those accounts used to live on `account.tax.group`).

### Worked example: a 20% VAT line

A product priced at 100.00 with one 20% percentage tax (`amount_type='percent'`, `price_include=False`):

1. The invoice line's `_get_computed_taxes` picks the product's `taxes_id`, filtered to the move's company ([account_move_line.py:1333](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1333)).
2. If the customer has a fiscal position (e.g. an EU intra-community position mapping 20% → 0% reverse charge), `map_tax` swaps it before the engine runs ([account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336)).
3. With domestic 20% kept: engine base = 100.00, tax = 20.00, total = 120.00. Had the tax been `price_include=True`, the 100.00 would be treated as gross: base = 83.33, tax = 16.67.
4. The invoice repartition lines split this: the `base` line (100%) carries the 100.00 to the income account with its base grid; the `tax` line (`factor_percent=100`) carries the 20.00 to the VAT account with its tax grid ([account_tax.py:5142](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5142)).
5. The 20.00 lands under the tax's group; at period end the **enterprise** tax closing nets all grids and posts the net to the `tax_payable_account_id` of the return type ([account_return.py:173](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L173)) — **changed in 20**, that account moved off `account.tax.group` into enterprise `account_reports` (see [Tax Report & Tax Closing Entry](#tax-report--tax-closing-entry)).

A reverse-charge tax reuses the same machinery with a `+100 / -100` repartition (the engine tracks this via `has_negative_factor`, [account_tax.py:199](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L199)): one tax line debits, an offsetting line credits, net VAT zero, but both grids fire so the amounts still show on the report. See [Reverse Charge](#reverse-charge-intra-eu--domestic).

### The engine in five stages (for developers)

The batch engine is not a single call — it is a pipeline every document runs in order. Knowing the stage names makes the rest of this doc navigable:

| # | Stage | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `_prepare_base_line_for_taxes_computation` | Package each line into a neutral "base line" dict: `tax_ids`, qty, price, partner, currency, conversion `rate`, `special_type` | [account_tax.py:1571](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1571) |
| 2 | `_add_tax_details_in_base_lines` | Run the math; attach a `tax_details` blob holding raw + rounded amounts in **both** document and company currency | [account_tax.py:1798](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1798) |
| 3 | `_round_base_lines_tax_details` | Round per currency; under round-globally, smooth the rounding delta across lines | [account_tax.py:2204](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2204) |
| 4 | `_add_accounting_data_in_base_lines_tax_details` | Apply repartition: resolve GL accounts, factors and tax-grid tags | [account_tax.py:2524](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2524) |
| 5 | `_prepare_tax_lines` | Diff against existing tax lines and emit add / update / delete for the journal items | [account_tax.py:3078](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3078) |

The three evaluation passes inside stage 2 always run in the same order: **fixed taxes first, then price-included (in reverse sequence), then price-excluded (forward)** ([account_tax.py:1195](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1195)) — see [Price-Included vs Price-Excluded](#price-included-vs-price-excluded-taxes).

### One engine, one set of numbers (manager assurance)

The most reassuring fact in the whole system: the totals shown on screen and the journal items that hit the ledger come from the **same** computation. `account.move` feeds both the on-screen `tax_totals` widget and the posted tax lines through the same `_get_rounded_base_and_tax_lines` pipeline ([account_move.py:1850](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1850), consumed by `_compute_tax_totals` at [account_move.py:1912](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1912)). And the Python engine is mirrored method-for-method in `account_tax.js` — every shared method carries a "KEEP BOTH METHODS CONSISTENT" banner ([account_tax.py:860](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L860)) — so the live in-browser preview matches the server. **What the customer is quoted is what posts to the books.**

### Gotchas

- **`price_include` is computed, not stored as a plain flag.** It derives from `price_include_override` on the tax and the company default `account_price_include` ([account_tax.py:299](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L299)), and — **new in 20** — from the document's own `document_tax_mode` when one is set ([account_tax.py:881](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L881)). Changing the company setting silently flips every tax that has no explicit override.
- **Invoice and refund repartition are two separate sets and must stay aligned.** A validation requires the same count, types, and `factor_percent` order between invoice and refund lines ([account_tax.py:511](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L511)). Edit one side and forget the other and the tax won't save.
- **`type_tax_use='none'` taxes can't be used alone** — they only work as children inside a `group` tax ([account_tax.py:66](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L66)).
- **Default rounding is `round_globally` ("Round per Tax"), not per line** ([company.py:133](../addons/account/models/company.py#L133)). This changes cent-level totals versus older intuition; switching to `round_per_line` can produce different invoice totals.
- **Tax-on-tax depends on two flags agreeing.** A lower-sequence tax must set `include_base_amount`, and the higher tax must keep `is_base_affected=True` (default), or the cascade silently does nothing ([account_tax.py:133](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L133), [account_tax.py:135](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L135)). Note `price_include` auto-enables `include_base_amount` via an onchange ([account_tax.py:679](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L679)).
- **An empty fiscal position is not a no-op.** A fiscal position with no `tax_ids` mapping (e.g. one created for a tax unit) removes *all* taxes from the line ([partner.py:173](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L173)).
- **Cash basis taxes need a transition account — but only the form enforces it (changed in 20).** The field is `required="tax_exigibility == 'on_payment'"` on the tax form ([account_tax_views.xml:261](../addons/account/views/account_tax_views.xml#L261)). The Python constraint that additionally demanded the account be reconcilable (`_constrains_cash_basis_transition_account`) was **removed in 20**, so a record written from code or an import can now carry `on_payment` with a missing or non-reconcilable transition account. The option itself only appears if the company enabled "Use Cash Basis" ([company.py:238](../addons/account/models/company.py#L238)).
- **Withholding is not in `account` core.** It lives in localization modules (`l10n_account_withholding_tax`, `l10n_ar_withholding`, etc.); do not expect a generic withholding flag on `account.tax`.
- **Tax grids are country-scoped.** A repartition line's `tag_ids` domain is restricted to the company's fiscal country (plus any foreign-VAT countries), [account_tax.py:5172](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5172) — grids from the wrong country won't be selectable.
- **Per-line tax cents may not sum to the printed total (round-globally default).** Under the default rounding the engine keeps full precision per line, rounds the document total once, then *smooths the rounding delta onto the largest line* ([account_tax.py:1848](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1848)). So a line can show a cent that looks "off" while the document total is the correct one. Don't re-add per-line cents to validate the total.
- **Cash-basis taxes carry no report tags on the invoice.** A tax set to "due on payment" gets its grid tags only later, on the entry generated when the invoice is paid — on the invoice itself the tax sits on a transition account with no tags ([account_tax.py:2388](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2388)). See [Cash-Basis Taxes](#cash-basis-taxes-tax-exigibility).

## The Tax Object: account.tax

**In plain terms:** a *tax* is the rule behind a single row like "15%" on the screen at **Accounting → Configuration → Taxes**. One record answers four questions — *where can it be picked* (Sales or Purchase), *how is the amount computed* (a percentage, a fixed fee, …), *how does it interact with other taxes on the same line*, and *which accounts and tax-return boxes the money goes to*. The [tax form, field by field](#the-tax-form-field-by-field) below maps every field you see on that screen to what it actually does.

Every percentage, fixed fee, or compound levy you see on an invoice, sales order, or product is a record of the `account.tax` model. One tax record answers four business questions at once: *Where can it be picked?* (`type_tax_use`, `tax_scope`), *How is the amount computed?* (`amount_type`, `amount`), *In what order and how does it interact with other taxes?* (`sequence`, `include_base_amount`, `is_base_affected`), and *Where does the money land in accounting?* (the repartition lines + `tax_group_id`). The model is defined as `class AccountTax` in [account_tax.py:56](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L56), ordered by `sequence,id` ([account_tax.py:60](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L60)).

### What a tax is *for* — where it shows up

A tax is not globally visible. `type_tax_use` ([account_tax.py:66](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L66)) is the master gate that decides which documents may use it:

| `type_tax_use` | Meaning | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| `sale` | Output tax (you charge it) | Product *Sales Taxes*, customer invoice lines, sale order lines |
| `purchase` | Input tax (a vendor charges you) | Product *Purchase Taxes*, vendor bill lines, purchase order lines |
| `none` | Cannot be selected on its own | Only usable as a child inside a *Group of Taxes* |

This filtering is not magic — it is plain domains wired into the consuming fields. The product's `taxes_id` (Sales Taxes) carries `domain=[('type_tax_use', '=', 'sale')]` and `supplier_taxes_id` (Purchase Taxes) carries `domain=[('type_tax_use', '=', 'purchase')]` ([product.py:82](../addons/account/models/product.py#L82), [product.py:89](../addons/account/models/product.py#L89)). Sale order lines restrict the same way ([sale_order_line.py:211](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L211)). When an invoice line auto-fills its taxes and the product has none, it falls back to the account's taxes filtered by direction: `sale` for customer documents, `purchase` for vendor documents ([account_move_line.py:1313](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1313), [account_move_line.py:1322](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1322)).

`tax_scope` ([account_tax.py:68](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L68)) narrows further by *product type*: `service` or `consu` (Goods). Leaving it empty means "applies to any product". It is a UI/selection filter; on the search view it powers the *Services* / *Goods* filters ([account_tax_views.xml:162](../addons/account/views/account_tax_views.xml#L162)). Inside a group, a child's scope must match the parent's scope or be empty, enforced by `_check_children_scope` ([account_tax.py:548](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L548)).

`active` ([account_tax.py:110](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L110)) lets you retire a tax without deleting it (history is preserved). `country_id` ([account_tax.py:182](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L182)) scopes the tax to a fiscal country; it defaults to the company's fiscal country and constrains which tax groups are selectable ([account_tax.py:280](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L280)).

### How the amount is computed — `amount_type`

`amount_type` ([account_tax.py:69](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L69)) picks the computation strategy. `amount` ([account_tax.py:118](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L118)) is the rate/value it consumes. The four values, at a glance (the math lives in its own section):

| `amount_type` | Behavior | Reads `amount`? |
|---|---|---|
| `percent` | Amount is a % of the base: `100 * (1 + 10%) = 110` | yes (as %) |
| `fixed` | Flat amount per unit, price-independent | yes (as currency) |
| `division` | "Percentage Tax Included": `180 / (1 - 10%) = 200` | yes (as %) |
| `group` | A container; the real taxes are its `children_tax_ids` | no |

For a `group`, `children_tax_ids` ([account_tax.py:112](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L112)) holds the sub-taxes (typically `type_tax_use='none'`). Children are applied in their own `sequence` order, nested groups are forbidden, and a child's `type_tax_use` must be `none` or equal the parent's ([account_tax.py:552](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L552), [account_tax.py:559](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L559)).

### Ordering and tax-on-tax cascading

When a line has several taxes, `sequence` ([account_tax.py:116](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L116)) sets the order they apply. Order matters because of two booleans that together implement *tax on tax*:

- `include_base_amount` ("Affect Base of Subsequent Taxes") ([account_tax.py:133](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L133)) — if set, this tax's amount is added into the base seen by every *later* tax (higher sequence) that accepts it.
- `is_base_affected` ("Base Affected by Previous Taxes") ([account_tax.py:135](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L135), default `True`, hidden under developer mode) — if set, this tax *agrees* to have its base inflated by earlier `include_base_amount` taxes.

The cascade only happens when the earlier tax says "push my amount into the base" *and* the later tax says "yes, affect me". In the computation engine, after a tax is processed, the subsequent-tax set is grown only when `tax.include_base_amount` is true ([account_tax.py:1269](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1269)), and a tax joins the "affected" set only when `tax.is_base_affected` is true ([account_tax.py:1278](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1278)). The price-excluded path that actually adds the extra base does so only for downstream taxes whose `is_base_affected` is true ([account_tax.py:1035](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1035)).

#### Worked cascade example

Two price-excluded sale taxes on a line of **100.00**:

| Tax | sequence | amount_type | amount | include_base_amount | is_base_affected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eco-fee | 1 | percent | 10% | **True** | — |
| VAT | 2 | percent | 20% | False | **True** |

Step by step:

1. Eco-fee runs first (sequence 1). Base = 100. Tax = `100 * 10% = 10.00`. Because `include_base_amount = True`, its 10.00 is pushed into the base of later affected taxes.
2. VAT runs next (sequence 2). It has `is_base_affected = True`, so its base becomes `100 + 10 = 110`. Tax = `110 * 20% = 22.00`.

Result: untaxed 100, Eco-fee 10.00, VAT 22.00, **total 132.00**. Had Eco-fee's `include_base_amount` been `False`, VAT would compute on 100 → 20.00 and the total would be 130.00. Had VAT's `is_base_affected` been `False`, it would also ignore the Eco-fee and compute on 100. The engine logic for this exact "tax affecting the base of subsequent ones, no price-included taxes" case is at [account_tax.py:1033-1038](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1033).

### Where the money lands — repartition and groups (introductions)

A tax does not just produce a number; it dictates *which accounts* receive it and in *what proportion*. That distribution is held in two One2many sets of `account.tax.repartition.line` ([account_tax.py:5135](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5135)):

- `invoice_repartition_line_ids` ([account_tax.py:160](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L160)) — distribution used on invoices.
- `refund_repartition_line_ids` ([account_tax.py:168](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L168)) — distribution used on credit notes/refunds.

Both are split by `document_type` (`invoice` vs `refund`) and feed the underlying `repartition_line_ids` ([account_tax.py:176](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L176)). Each line says "send X% of the base/tax to account Y with these tax tags", which is what enables splits like reverse charge (+100 / -100) and grid reporting. Depth on repartition lives in the repartition section.

`tax_group_id` ([account_tax.py:141](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L141)) links the tax to an `account.tax.group` (defined at [account_tax.py:25](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L25)). The group is how taxes are *subtotaled and labelled* on documents. **Changed in 20:** it no longer carries the tax-closing payable/receivable accounts — those moved to the enterprise `account.return.type` ([account_return.py:173](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L173)). It is required (auto-computed from country+company, [account_tax.py:280](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L280)) and must share the tax's country ([account_tax.py:248](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L248)). Groups get their own section.

### Gotchas

- **`type_tax_use='none'` taxes are invisible everywhere except inside a group.** That is by design — the help text on the field says so ([account_tax.py:67](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L67)). Build group children as `none`.
- **The cascade needs *both* booleans.** `include_base_amount` on the upstream tax and `is_base_affected` on the downstream tax. Setting only one does nothing ([account_tax.py:1035](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1035), [account_tax.py:1278](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1278)).
- **`is_base_affected` is hidden unless you are in developer mode** (`groups="base.group_no_one"`, [account_tax_views.xml:255](../addons/account/views/account_tax_views.xml#L255)) and defaults to `True`. Most users never see it, so taxes silently accept cascading.
- **Sequence drives the cascade, not creation order.** Reordering taxes by `sequence` changes which one feeds the other's base. The group children list reorders via the handle widget ([account_tax_views.xml:231](../addons/account/views/account_tax_views.xml#L231)).
- **Tax names must be unique** per company + `type_tax_use` + `tax_scope` + `country_id` (for non-`none` taxes), enforced by `_constrains_name` ([account_tax.py:219](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L219)). Two 20% VATs for different countries are fine; two identical ones are not.
- **No nested groups.** A group's child cannot itself be `amount_type='group'` ([account_tax.py:559](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L559)).
- **A group has no `tax_group_id`, `amount`, or repartition of its own** — those belong to its children; the form hides them for groups. The group tax is purely a container.
- **`tax_group_id` must match `country_id`.** Changing a tax's country can invalidate its group ([account_tax.py:248](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L248)); the field domain restricts groups to the same country or no country ([account_tax.py:146](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L146)).
- **`price_include` is no longer a stored boolean you set directly** — it is computed from `price_include_override` plus the company default `account_price_include` ([account_tax.py:299](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L299)). To force tax-included on one tax, set `price_include_override`, not `price_include`. **New in 20:** a document can override both through `document_tax_mode`, resolved in `_is_price_included` ([account_tax.py:881](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L881)).
- **Empty `tax_scope` means "all products", not "no products".** Easy to misread as a restriction when it is actually the permissive default.

## Tax Computation Methods (Percent, Fixed, Group, Division, Python)

**In plain terms:** the **Tax Computation** dropdown picks the formula. Use **Percentage** for normal VAT/sales tax (a rate on the net), **Fixed** for a flat per-unit levy (eco-fee, excise per bottle), **Percentage of Price Tax Included** (`division`) when the law expresses the tax as a slice of the gross price, and **Group of Taxes** to stack several taxes under one label (state + county + city). A fifth, **Custom Formula** (`code`), comes from an optional module for rules that fit none of the above.

Every Odoo tax (`account.tax`) declares **how** it turns a line amount into a tax figure. That choice lives in the `amount_type` selection field, with a matching numeric `amount`. The four built-in methods are defined in [account_tax.py:69-80](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L69): `group`, `fixed`, `percent`, and `division` ("Percentage Tax Included"). The `account_tax_python` module bolts on a fifth, `code` (Custom Formula), in [account_tax.py:15-18](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L15).

This section explains what each method computes, why it exists, and how the shared engine ties them together.

### The shared engine: where computation actually happens

All methods funnel through one private method, `_get_tax_details` ([account_tax.py:1118](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1118)). It is the heart of the engine. The per-method math is split across three small helpers it calls in a fixed order:

| Helper | Handles | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `_eval_tax_amount_fixed_amount` | `fixed` (and `code` via override) | [account_tax.py:1063](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1063) |
| `_eval_tax_amount_price_included` | `percent`/`division` when tax is in the price | [account_tax.py:1078](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1078) |
| `_eval_tax_amount_price_excluded` | `percent`/`division` when tax is on top | [account_tax.py:1098](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1098) |

> **Changed in 20 — `document_tax_mode`.** `_get_tax_details`, `_batch_for_taxes_computation`, `compute_all`, `_adapt_price_unit_to_another_taxes` and `_fix_tax_included_price` all gained a `document_tax_mode` keyword. Whether a tax is treated as price-included is no longer read straight off `tax.price_include`; every call site goes through the new `_is_price_included(document_tax_mode, special_mode)` helper ([account_tax.py:881](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L881)). Method names and the rest of the signatures are unchanged.

`_get_tax_details` first computes the raw base `raw_base = quantity * price_unit` ([account_tax.py:1211](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1211)), then evaluates taxes in three passes: fixed taxes first (they can shift the base of a following price-included batch), then price-included taxes descending, then price-excluded taxes ascending ([account_tax.py:1233-1242](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1233)). It returns a dict of `total_excluded`, `total_included`, and a `taxes_data` list with per-tax `base` and `tax_amount` ([account_tax.py:1152-1160](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1152)).

### percent — a flat rate on the base

Business meaning: standard VAT/sales tax. `amount` is the percentage. Tax = base × rate/100.

Price-excluded (tax added on top): [account_tax.py:1110-1111](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1110):
```
return raw_base * self.amount / 100.0
```

Price-included (tax already inside the price): the engine first strips the tax out of the base. With a single 21% included tax, `total_percentage = 0.21`, so `to_price_excluded_factor = 1/1.21`, and the tax is `raw_base × (1/1.21) × 21/100` ([account_tax.py:1090-1093](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1090)).

**Worked example — 100 @ 21%, tax-excluded:**
- `raw_base = 1 × 100 = 100`
- tax = `100 × 21/100 = 21.00`
- `total_excluded = 100.00`, `total_included = 121.00`

**Same tax, price-included on 121:** factor `1/1.21`, tax = `121 × (1/1.21) × 0.21 = 21.00`, base = `121 − 21 = 100.00`. Same result, reached backwards.

### fixed — a flat amount per unit

Business meaning: per-unit levies that don't scale with price — eco-tax, recycling fee, excise per bottle. `amount` is the charge **per unit**; it is multiplied by `quantity`, not by price ([account_tax.py:1074-1076](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1074)):
```
sign = -1 if evaluation_context['price_unit'] < 0.0 else 1
return sign * evaluation_context['quantity'] * self.amount
```
The `sign` only mirrors the price's sign (for refunds/credit notes); magnitude is independent of price.

**Worked example — eco-tax 0.30 per unit, qty 5:**
- tax = `1 × 5 × 0.30 = 1.50` (the unit price is irrelevant to the amount)
- On a product priced 20.00 with this single fixed tax: `total_excluded = 100.00`, tax `1.50`, `total_included = 101.50`.

Fixed taxes evaluate **first** so that, if flagged "Affect Base of Subsequent Taxes" (`include_base_amount`, [account_tax.py:133](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L133)), the 1.50 can be folded into the base a later percent tax sees.

### division — percentage of the tax-inclusive total

Business meaning: "the price already contains tax, and the tax is X% of the **gross** (tax-included) total." Common in fuel and some retail regimes where the law expresses the levy as a fraction of the all-in price rather than of the net.

Price-included form (the natural one): tax = gross × amount/100 ([account_tax.py:1095-1096](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1095)):
```
return raw_base * self.amount / 100.0
```

Price-excluded form (back-compute the gross from a net): divide by `(1 − rate)` first ([account_tax.py:1113-1116](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1113)):
```
incl_base_multiplicator = 1.0 if total_percentage == 1.0 else 1 - total_percentage
return raw_base * self.amount / 100.0 / incl_base_multiplicator
```

The contrast with `percent` is the divisor. The field help spells it out at [account_tax.py:77-79](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L77): `180 / (1 − 10%) = 200` excluded; `200 × (1 − 10%) = 180` included.

**Worked example — 10% division, price-included, gross 200:**
- tax = `200 × 10/100 = 20.00`
- net base = `200 − 20 = 180.00`
- So the tax is 10% **of 200**, not of 180. A 10% *percent* tax on the same 180 net would have been 18.00 — division yields more because the percentage applies to the larger inclusive figure.

### group — a parent that delegates to children

Business meaning: one label on the invoice ("Sales Tax 8.875%") that is really several jurisdiction taxes (state + county + city) stacked together. A group tax has no math of its own; it holds `children_tax_ids` ([account_tax.py:112](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L112)) and contributes nothing directly.

Before any computation, the engine **flattens** the hierarchy. `flatten_taxes_hierarchy` ([account_tax.py:4839](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4839)) delegates to `_flatten_taxes_and_sort_them` ([account_tax.py:853](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L853)), which replaces each group with its children, ordered by sequence:
```
if tax.amount_type == 'group':
    children = tax.children_tax_ids.sorted(key=sort_key)
    sorted_taxes |= children
```
So `[G, B([A, D, F]), E, C]` evaluates as `[A, D, F, C, E, G]` ([account_tax.py:857](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L857)). Each child is then computed independently by its own `amount_type`; the group only survives as a back-reference (`group_per_tax`) so the UI can re-aggregate the children under the parent's name.

**Worked example — group of 5% + 3% (both percent, excluded) on 100:**
- child 5%: `100 × 0.05 = 5.00`
- child 3%: `100 × 0.03 = 3.00`
- group total tax = 8.00, `total_included = 108.00`. There is no "8%" computed anywhere — only the two children, summed.

### code / python — a custom formula

Business meaning: levies that don't fit a rate, fee, or division — e.g. weight- or attribute-based taxes, tiered fees, "max of two rules." Provided by the `account_tax_python` module, which adds the `code` value and a `formula` text field (default `price_unit * 0.10`) at [account_tax.py:19-27](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L19).

It hooks into the engine by **overriding the fixed-amount step** ([account_tax.py:116-120](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L116)): a `code` tax behaves like a fixed tax (evaluated first), but instead of `quantity × amount` it evaluates the formula via `_eval_tax_amount_formula` ([account_tax.py:85](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L85)).

Variables available in the formula context ([account_tax.py:85-104](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L85)):

| Variable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `base` | the raw base passed in (`raw_base + extra_base_for_tax`) |
| `price_unit` | line unit price |
| `quantity` | line quantity |
| `product` | dict of whitelisted, non-relational product fields |
| `uom` | dict of whitelisted UoM fields |

**Worked example — formula `max(price_unit * 0.05, 2.0)`:**
- price 100 → `max(5.0, 2.0) = 5.00`
- price 20 → `max(1.0, 2.0) = 2.00` (the floor wins)

**Security note.** The formula is **not** arbitrary Python. It is parsed to an AST and validated by `check_formula` / `TaxFormulaValidator` ([formula_utils.py:120](../addons/account_tax_python/tools/formula_utils.py#L120), [formula_utils.py:66](../addons/account_tax_python/tools/formula_utils.py#L66)). Only a node whitelist is allowed — names limited to `price_unit, quantity, base, product, uom` ([formula_utils.py:9](../addons/account_tax_python/tools/formula_utils.py#L9)), function calls limited to `min`/`max` ([formula_utils.py:8](../addons/account_tax_python/tools/formula_utils.py#L8)), arithmetic/comparison/boolean ops, and constants of type int/float/None only. `product.foo` is rewritten to `product['foo']` and the field must be **non-relational and serializable** ([account_tax.py:72-75](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L72)). Evaluation runs through `expr_eval` on a JSON-roundtripped context that rejects non-primitive types ([account_tax.py:107-114](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L107)) — **changed in 20**, `safe_eval` was swapped for the narrower `expr_eval` now that `odoo.tools.safe_eval` is a package. This dual restriction exists so the same formula can run identically in the Python backend and the JavaScript frontend.

### `compute_all` is a shim; the batch engine does the work

There are *not* two co-equal engines. The real engine is the **batch / base-line** path; `compute_all` ([account_tax.py:4848](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4848)) is a thin **backward-compatibility shim** — you call it on a tax recordset with a `price_unit` and `quantity`, and internally it builds one base line, calls the same modern engine, and reshapes the result into the old dict ([account_tax.py:4929-4952](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4929)). `account.move` and POS posting never call it; they use the batch path below. `compute_all` survives only in a few single-amount UX/helper spots (the product `tax_string` price hint, gross-price helpers such as `_get_gross_price_unit`, some price-included fallbacks). Its return dict ([account_tax.py:4959-4965](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4959)):

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `total_excluded` | net base, all taxes removed |
| `total_included` | gross, all taxes applied |
| `total_void` | portion of tax on repartition lines with no account set |
| `base_tags` | tax-grid tags for the base line |
| `taxes` | one dict per tax/repartition line: `id`, `name`, `amount`, `base`, `account_id`, `tag_ids`, `group`, … |

The newer, preferred path for documents is the **batch engine**: `_add_tax_details_in_base_line` ([account_tax.py:1723](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1723)) and its plural `_add_tax_details_in_base_lines` ([account_tax.py:1798](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1798)). Invoices, sale orders, and POS prepare a list of "base lines," attach a `tax_details` blob to each (with raw amounts in both company and foreign currency), then round across the whole document at once via `_round_tax_details_tax_amounts` ([account_tax.py:1916](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1916)). This batch approach is what lets `round_globally` spread sub-cent rounding deltas correctly over many lines — something a per-line `compute_all` cannot do.

### Gotchas

- **`amount` reuse.** The single `amount` field ([account_tax.py:118](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L118)) means percentage for `percent`/`division` but a per-unit currency value for `fixed`. Reading `amount` without knowing `amount_type` is meaningless.
- **`fixed` is per-unit, not per-line.** It is multiplied by `quantity`. Setting `amount = 0.30` on a 10-unit line gives 3.00, not 0.30.
- **`division` percentage applies to the gross.** It is *not* the same as a percent tax on the net base; for the same rate `division` always yields a larger tax because the denominator is the inclusive total.
- **A group tax never computes anything itself.** It is flattened away before math; only its children are evaluated. Putting an `amount` on a group has no effect.
- **`code` taxes ride the fixed-tax pass.** They are evaluated in the first (fixed) loop ([account_tax.py:118-122](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L118)), so a formula tax can affect a later price-included batch's base if `include_base_amount` is set — just like a fixed tax.
- **Formula cannot touch relational fields.** `product.partner_id.name` is rejected; only non-relational, JSON-serializable product/UoM fields are allowed ([account_tax.py:72-75](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L72)). Division-by-zero in a formula silently returns 0.0 ([account_tax.py:113-114](../addons/account_tax_python/models/account_tax.py#L113)).
- **Rounding is method-dependent.** `_get_tax_details` rounds the base and each tax per line under `round_per_line` ([account_tax.py:1164](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1164), [account_tax.py:1212](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1212)); under `round_globally` the deltas are deferred to the document-level batch reconciliation. Mixing the two on the same document produces different cent-level totals.
- **`compute_all` is single-line.** For multi-line documents it cannot reproduce global rounding; use the base-line batch engine instead.
- **Both Python and JS implementations must match.** Nearly every engine method carries a "Mirror of the same method in account_tax.js — PLZ KEEP BOTH CONSISTENT" banner (e.g. [account_tax.py:1133-1134](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1133)). Changing tax math in Python without mirroring it in `account_tax.js` makes the on-screen preview diverge from the posted entry.

## Price-Included vs Price-Excluded Taxes

In Odoo a tax is either *on top of* the price (price-excluded) or *baked into* it (price-included). A retailer in a VAT country usually quotes shelf prices that already contain VAT, while a B2B supplier quotes net prices and adds VAT on the invoice. The same product, the same 21% tax, but the number the salesperson types means something different. This section explains how Odoo decides which interpretation applies, how it back-computes the net base when the price already contains the tax, and where the "(= 121 Incl. Taxes)" hint under a product price comes from.

> **Decisions & risks (managers):** Whether prices include tax is a **company-wide default** ([company.py:294](../addons/account/models/company.py#L294)). **Changed in 20:** it is no longer frozen after the first posted entry — the constraint `_check_set_account_price_include` was removed, because every invoice, sale order and purchase order now stores its own `document_tax_mode` and keeps it. Changing the company setting therefore only affects documents created afterwards; existing documents are untouched. It only changes the *number people type and see*; it does **not** change the net revenue booked or the VAT reported (100 net + 21 VAT either way). Retail/B2C usually quotes tax-included round prices; B2B usually quotes net. Per-tax overrides and the per-document switch handle the exceptions.

### The two layers: company default and per-tax override

Odoo splits the decision into two layers so the common case needs zero per-tax setup, and only exceptions are overridden.

**Layer 1 — company default.** Each company carries `account_price_include`, a Selection of `tax_included` / `tax_excluded`, defaulting to `tax_excluded` ([company.py:294](../addons/account/models/company.py#L294)). It is exposed in Accounting Settings as *Default Sales Price Include* via a related field ([res_config_settings.py:53](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L53), radio widget at [res_config_settings_views.xml:79](../addons/account/views/res_config_settings_views.xml#L79)). **Changed in 20:** it is a pure default now — the v19 constraint that blocked changing it once accounting entries existed is gone, since each document freezes its own mode (layer 3 below).

**Layer 2 — per-tax override.** Each tax has `price_include_override`, also `tax_included` / `tax_excluded`, but it is *optional* (no default, so it can be empty) ([account_tax.py:127](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L127)). Empty means "inherit the company default". The company default itself is mirrored onto the tax as the read-only related field `company_price_include` ([account_tax.py:126](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L126)) so the resolution can happen on the tax record.

**Layer 3 — per-document mode (new in 20).** `account.move`, `sale.order` and `purchase.order` each carry a stored `document_tax_mode` (`tax_excluded` / `tax_included`), seeded from `company.account_price_include` when the document is created and editable afterwards ([account_move.py:641](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L641), computed at [account_move.py:2558](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L2558); a SQL constraint forces it to be set on every invoice/receipt, [account_move.py:836](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L836)). When a document passes its mode into the engine it **wins over the company default but still loses to an explicit per-tax `price_include_override`** — the precedence is resolved in `_is_price_included` ([account_tax.py:881](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L881)).

The actual behavior is driven by a single computed boolean, `price_include`, never by the Selections directly ([account_tax.py:122](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L122)). Its resolution — for the tax alone, ignoring any document mode — is:

```python
tax.price_include = (
    tax.price_include_override == 'tax_included'
    or (tax.company_price_include == 'tax_included'
        and not tax.price_include_override)
)
```

— [account_tax.py:299-305](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L299).

| `price_include_override` | `company_price_include` | resulting `price_include` |
|---|---|---|
| `tax_included` | (anything) | True |
| `tax_excluded` | (anything) | False |
| empty | `tax_included` | True |
| empty | `tax_excluded` | False |

So the override wins when set; otherwise the company default decides. On a real document the resolution order is `special_mode` → `price_include_override` → `document_tax_mode` → `tax.price_include` ([account_tax.py:881-900](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L881)). `price_include` is computed (not stored), with a custom `_search_price_include` ([account_tax.py:307](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L307)) — but it is **narrow**: it only handles `in` / `not in` and asserts the value is exactly `[True]` ([account_tax.py:307-316](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L307)). So `[('price_include','=',True)]` works (the ORM normalizes `=` to `in [True]`), but searching for `False` or using any other operator returns `NotImplemented`. To filter robustly, query the underlying `price_include_override` / `company_price_include` fields instead.

### Why a computed boolean instead of just a checkbox

Older Odoo had a plain `price_include` checkbox on each tax. The two-layer design lets an administrator flip a whole company from net to gross pricing in one setting, without editing every tax. The override exists for the genuine exceptions (e.g. one excise tax that must stay excluded even in a tax-included company). The boolean is the single source of truth the computation engine reads, so the rest of the code never has to think about the two Selections.

### How the net base is back-computed (percent, price-included)

When a tax is price-included, the price the user typed (`price_unit * quantity`, the `raw_base`) is the *gross* figure. Odoo must peel the tax back out to find the net base for the GL.

For `amount_type='percent'`, the price-included tax amount is ([account_tax.py:1090-1093](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1090)):

```python
total_percentage = sum(tax.amount for tax in batch) / 100.0
to_price_excluded_factor = 1 / (1 + total_percentage)
return raw_base * to_price_excluded_factor * self.amount / 100.0
```

This is the classic gross-to-net unwind. With a single 21% tax and gross 121:

- `total_percentage = 0.21`, factor `= 1 / 1.21 = 0.826446…`
- tax = `121 × 0.826446 × 0.21 = 21.00`

The net base is then produced separately. In the main loop, for a price-included tax (with no special mode), the base is the raw base minus the batch's tax amount ([account_tax.py:1262-1264](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1262)):

```python
base = raw_base + tax_data['extra_base_for_base']
if tax_data['price_include'] and special_mode in (False, 'total_included'):
    base -= total_tax_amount
```

So `base = 121 − 21 = 100`. Math: gross 121 ÷ 1.21 = 100 net, tax 21.

Contrast with a **price-excluded** percent tax, which is trivial — the base *is* the raw base and the tax sits on top ([account_tax.py:1111](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1111)): `return raw_base * self.amount / 100.0`. Net 100 → tax 21 → gross 121. Same end totals, different starting number.

### `special_mode`: forcing the interpretation

The back-computation code guards on `special_mode` ([account_tax.py:1142](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1142)) — an internal three-state flag the engine uses to *override* each tax's own `price_include` for one computation:

| `special_mode` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `False` (normal) | Honour each tax's own `price_include`. |
| `'total_included'` | Treat the given amount as the **gross** (tax-included) total, whatever the taxes say. |
| `'total_excluded'` | Treat it as the **net**, and add tax on top. |

This is how features that have *already* decided whether an amount is net or gross — down payments, global discounts, non-deductible base lines — re-run the engine against it. `compute_all`'s `handle_price_include` argument and the `force_price_include` context both map onto it ([account_tax.py:4902-4907](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4902)). It also outranks `document_tax_mode`: `_is_price_included` checks `special_mode` before anything else ([account_tax.py:892-896](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L892)). You won't set it by hand, but you'll meet it in the engine and in base-line dicts.

### Order of evaluation

`compute_all` processes taxes in three passes ([account_tax.py:1233-1242](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1233)): fixed taxes first (ascending), then price-included taxes in **reverse** order, then price-excluded taxes in normal order. Price-included taxes are computed before the base is known precisely because the engine must subtract them from the raw base to reveal the net.

### `amount_type='division'` — the "tax included in price" type

`division` ("Percentage Tax Included") models taxes that are legally defined as a fraction *of the gross*, not of the net — common for some withholding and retail levies. The help text spells it out: `180 / (1 - 10%) = 200` ([account_tax.py:69](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L69)).

The two modes differ:

- **Price-included division** ([account_tax.py:1095](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1095)): the gross already contains the tax, so the amount is simply `raw_base * self.amount / 100.0`. On gross 200 at 10% → tax = 20, base = 180.
- **Price-excluded division** ([account_tax.py:1113](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1113)): the typed price is net, and the tax is grossed up by dividing:

```python
incl_base_multiplicator = 1 - total_percentage      # = 0.9 for 10%
return raw_base * self.amount / 100.0 / incl_base_multiplicator
```

On net 180 at 10% → `180 × 0.10 / 0.9 = 20`. The key distinction from `percent`: division computes the tax as a slice of the *whole including itself*, where percent computes it as a slice of the net only. 10% percent on 180 = 18; 10% division on 180 (excluded) = 20.

### `include_base_amount` cascading with price-included taxes

`include_base_amount` ("Affect Base of Subsequent Taxes") makes a tax's amount fold into the base of later (higher-sequence) taxes that accept it via `is_base_affected` ([account_tax.py:135](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L135)). This is how an eco-tax or duty becomes taxable by VAT.

The tricky case is a **fixed tax that includes its base, sitting under a price-included percent tax**. The source comment works it through ([account_tax.py:1225-1232](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1225)):

> t1: fixed tax of 1, include base amount. t2: 21% price included. Price unit 121. Base of t1 = 121 / 1.1 = 110.

The fixed pass runs first so t1's amount is known before t2 unwinds the price; the engine then adds t1's amount back when reconstructing t2's base. In the main loop a `subsequent_taxes` accumulator carries each `is_base_affected` tax forward, and any tax with `include_base_amount` attaches those downstream taxes ([account_tax.py:1269](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1269)). Order matters: fixed → price-included (reverse) → price-excluded (forward).

### Where the displayed product price hint comes from

On the product form you see a grey hint like `(= 121.00 Incl. Taxes)` next to the sales price. That is `tax_string`, computed on both `product.template` and `product.product` ([product.py:85](../addons/account/models/product.py#L85), [product.py:335](../addons/account/models/product.py#L335)). The template version computes off `list_price`, the variant off `lst_price` ([product.py:221](../addons/account/models/product.py#L221), [product.py:445](../addons/account/models/product.py#L445)).

Both call `_construct_tax_string(price)` ([product.py:225](../addons/account/models/product.py#L225)). It runs the product's sales taxes through `compute_all` and shows whichever total *differs* from the entered price:

```python
included = res['total_included']
if currency.compare_amounts(included, price):
    joined.append(_('%(amount)s Incl. Taxes', amount=...))
excluded = res['total_excluded']
if currency.compare_amounts(excluded, price):
    joined.append(_('%(amount)s Excl. Taxes', amount=...))
```

So if you typed 100 with a price-excluded 21% tax, the hint reads `(= 121.00 Incl. Taxes)`. If you typed 121 with a price-included 21% tax, it reads `(= 100.00 Excl. Taxes)`. The matching total is suppressed because it equals what you already see in the field.

### Worked example: 121 tax-incl at 21% on a sale order line

Product sales price = **121.00**, one sales tax of 21% with `price_include = True` (either the tax overrides to `tax_included`, or the company default is `tax_included`). One unit on a sale order line.

1. `raw_base = quantity × price_unit = 1 × 121 = 121` ([account_tax.py:1211](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1211)).
2. Tax is price-included, percent pass runs: `121 × (1/1.21) × 0.21 = 21.00` ([account_tax.py:1090-1093](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1090)).
3. Base = `121 − 21 = 100.00` ([account_tax.py:1264](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1264)).

The sale order line shows:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Unit Price | 121.00 |
| Untaxed (`total_excluded`) | 100.00 |
| Tax | 21.00 |
| Total (`total_included`) | 121.00 |

The order's *Untaxed Amount* subtotal is 100.00 and *Total* is 121.00 — the customer pays exactly the 121 you quoted, and the GL books 100 to revenue and 21 to the VAT account. Had the tax been price-excluded, the same 121 unit price would have produced untaxed 121, tax 25.41, total 146.41.

### Gotchas

- **The boolean, not the Selection, drives behavior.** Reading `price_include_override` alone is wrong — an empty override still yields price-included if the company default is `tax_included`. Always read the computed `price_include` ([account_tax.py:299](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L299)) — or, inside the engine, call `_is_price_included(document_tax_mode)` ([account_tax.py:881](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L881)), which is the only place that also honours the document's own mode.
- **Changed in 20: the company default is no longer locked after invoicing.** The v19 constraint `_check_set_account_price_include` was removed. Flipping `account_price_include` now only changes the default for *new* documents — every existing invoice, sale order and purchase order keeps the `document_tax_mode` it was created with, so historical totals do not move. The flip side: two documents in the same company can legitimately disagree about whether prices include tax, so read `document_tax_mode` on the record, never the company setting, when reasoning about an existing document.
- **`division` ≠ `percent` price-included.** Both are "tax in the price," but division taxes the whole (including itself) while percent taxes the net. Same rate, different amount: 10% on 180 gives 18 (percent) vs 20 (division).
- **`include_base_amount` only flows to taxes that accept it.** The downstream tax must have `is_base_affected = True` ([account_tax.py:135](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L135)); otherwise the cascade silently does nothing.
- **Evaluation order is fixed and non-obvious.** Fixed taxes compute first, then price-included in *reverse* sequence, then price-excluded forward ([account_tax.py:1233-1242](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1233)). A fixed `include_base_amount` tax under a price-included tax relies on this ordering to compute its base correctly.
- **The Python `compute_all` has a JS twin.** `account_tax.py` repeatedly warns its methods mirror `account_tax.js` ([account_tax.py:1133](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1133)); editing one side without the other desyncs the live POS/web tax preview from the server.
- **`tax_string` hides the matching total.** It only shows a line if the computed total *differs* from the entered price ([product.py:233-237](../addons/account/models/product.py#L233)). A blank-looking hint can mean there are no taxes set, not that tax is zero.
- **Display base for price-included division is special-cased.** The tax-totals summary adds the division tax amount back into the displayed base ([account_tax.py:2873-2888](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2873)), so the "base" shown on division-tax documents is the gross-inclusive figure, not the bare `total_excluded`.

## Tax Distribution & Tax Grids (Repartition Lines + Tags)

A tax in Odoo does not just compute an amount. It also decides **where that amount lands** (which GL accounts) and **which boxes of the legal tax return it feeds** (the tax grids). Both of those decisions live in *repartition lines* — the rows you see under the **Distribution for Invoices** / **Distribution for Refunds** tabs of a tax. This is what lets one VAT tax post part of itself to a deductible account, part to a non-deductible one, and tag the base and the tax into the correct report boxes — all automatically, every time the tax is used.

### The two repartition sets, and why a tax needs both

Every `account.tax` carries two ordered lists of repartition lines:

| Field | Used when | Defined at |
|---|---|---|
| `invoice_repartition_line_ids` | tax appears on an invoice / vendor bill | [account_tax.py:160](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L160) |
| `refund_repartition_line_ids` | tax appears on a credit note / refund | [account_tax.py:168](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L168) |

The reason for two sets is that a refund is **not** simply an invoice with flipped signs at the grid level. A country's tax return often has a *different box* for "VAT on sales" versus "VAT corrected via credit notes". Splitting invoice vs refund repartition lets each direction point its base and tax to the right report box and, if needed, a different account. The selector is `document_type` on the line itself (`invoice` / `refund`) — see [account_tax.py:5151](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5151).

When you create a tax and leave these empty, Odoo auto-seeds each set with a minimal pair — one `base` line and one `tax` line, both at 100%, no tags — via `_compute_invoice_repartition_line_ids` / `_compute_refund_repartition_line_ids` ([account_tax.py:439](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L439), [account_tax.py:448](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L448)). `repartition_line_ids` ([account_tax.py:176](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L176)) is just the union of both, used internally.

A hard constraint keeps the two sets aligned: they must have the **same number of lines**, in the **same order**, with **matching `factor_percent` per row**, and each must have exactly one `base` line and at least one `tax` line. See `_validate_repartition_lines` at [account_tax.py:511](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L511) and `_check_repartition_lines` at [account_tax.py:503](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L503). This is why, in the UI, editing one set tends to mirror the structure of the other.

### Anatomy of a repartition line

The model is `account.tax.repartition.line`, defined at [account_tax.py:5135](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5135).

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `repartition_type` | `base` or `tax`. A `base` line tags the **net/base** amount; a `tax` line distributes the **computed tax** amount. [account_tax.py:5150](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5150) |
| `factor_percent` / `factor` | Percentage of the amount this line takes. `factor = factor_percent / 100` ([account_tax.py:5187](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5187)). 12 decimals of precision. |
| `account_id` | The GL account the tax amount is posted to. **Only meaningful for `tax` lines** — selecting `base` clears it ([account_tax.py:5191-5194](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5191)). If empty, the amount stays on the base line's own account. [account_tax.py:5152](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5152) |
| `tag_ids` | The **tax grids** (`account.account.tag`, applicability `taxes`) stamped onto the resulting move line. These are the `+21` / `-21` style boxes. [account_tax.py:5159](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5159) |
| `use_in_tax_closing` | Whether this line's account is swept into the periodic tax closing entry. [account_tax.py:5164](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5164) |
| `document_type` | `invoice` or `refund` — which set this line belongs to. |
| `sequence` | Display + matching order. Invoice and refund lines pair up by position, so order matters. |

`use_in_tax_closing` is computed but overridable: it defaults to `True` only for a `tax` line whose account is **not** an income/expense account (i.e. a real VAT control account), via `_compute_use_in_tax_closing` ([account_tax.py:5178](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5178)). A non-deductible portion booked to an expense account is therefore excluded from tax closing by default — which is correct, because that VAT became a cost, not a receivable/payable with the tax authority.

### Splitting one tax across accounts and percentages

Because each direction is a *list*, a single tax can fan out into several `tax` lines. Two classic patterns:

- **Partial deductibility (e.g. 50% deductible VAT on cars).** Two `tax` lines, each `factor_percent = 50`. One points to the deductible VAT account (`use_in_tax_closing = True`); the other points to an expense account so the non-deductible half becomes a cost. Both can carry different grids so the return reports each half separately.
- **Reverse charge (intra-EU).** The base is taxed at, say, 21%, but the buyer must declare both the VAT they owe and the VAT they reclaim. This is modelled with a `+100%` tax line and a `-100%` tax line — net cash effect zero, but two grid entries (one positive box, one negative box).

The `+100 / -100` reverse-charge shape is explicitly supported. The validation in [account_tax.py:539-545](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L539) sums positive-factor `tax` lines (must total +100%) **and** negative-factor `tax` lines (must total -100%) independently. The flag `has_negative_factor` ([account_tax.py:199](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L199), computed at [account_tax.py:457](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L457)) tells the engine a tax has such a negative leg.

### How tags reach the move lines and feed the report

Grids are stamped during tax computation, in `_add_accounting_data_to_base_line_tax_details` ([account_tax.py:2388](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2388)). Two distinct things happen:

**1. Base-line tags.** The net amount line collects the `base`-type repartition tags of every tax applied to it ([account_tax.py:2431-2432](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2431)). The correct set (invoice vs refund) is chosen by `is_refund` at [account_tax.py:2412-2415](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2412). Product tags are merged in too ([account_tax.py:2422-2425](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2422)).

**2. Tax-line tags.** Each generated tax line gets the `tag_ids` of the specific `tax` repartition line that produced it ([account_tax.py:2501-2504](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2501)). For a reverse-charge tax, the engine picks the negative-factor reps when the tax_data is flagged `is_reverse_charge` and the positive ones otherwise ([account_tax.py:2435-2440](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2435)), so the `+VAT` and `-VAT` legs land on separate lines with their own boxes.

Both flow onto the journal item through `tax_tag_ids` on `account.move.line` ([account_move_line.py:267](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L267)). The grouping key that builds each tax line carries `tax_tag_ids` and `tax_repartition_line_id` ([account_tax.py:2361](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2361), [account_tax.py:2350](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2350)). That `tax_repartition_line_id` back-pointer ([account_move_line.py:259](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L259)) is the audit trail: it records *which* repartition line created the line, and it's also what `_compute_is_refund` reads to decide direction on manual journal entries ([account_move_line.py:1562](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1562)).

The tax report then sums `balance` of all move lines carrying a given grid. A grid name beginning with `-` (e.g. report formula `-21`) means the report reads `-balance` for that box — captured by the tag's `balance_negate` flag ([account_account_tag.py:20](../addons/account/models/account_account_tag.py#L20)). **Changed in 20:** `balance_negate` and `report_expression_id` are now non-stored computed fields with a `compute_sql` twin ([account_account_tag.py:65](../addons/account/models/account_account_tag.py#L65)), so a report can filter and group on them in SQL without storing them. This is the sign mechanism behind `+21` / `-21` boxes.

### Worked example: 21% reverse-charge purchase

Vendor bill, intra-EU purchase, net 1,000.00, reverse-charge VAT 21%. The tax has these `tax` repartition lines (invoice set):

| Rep line | type | factor | account | grids |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RL-base | base | 100% | (none) | `+82` (taxable base, EU) |
| RL-vat+ | tax | +100% | VAT payable | `+55` (VAT due) |
| RL-vat- | tax | -100% | VAT deductible | `-59` (VAT reclaimed) |

Computation: tax amount = 1,000 × 21% = 210.00. The engine applies each `tax` line's factor ([account_tax.py:2443-2494](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2443)):

- RL-vat+ → +210.00 to **VAT payable**, tagged `+55`
- RL-vat- → -210.00 to **VAT deductible**, tagged `-59`

Resulting journal entry:

| Line | Debit | Credit | tax_tag_ids |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expense (base) | 1,000.00 | | `+82` |
| VAT payable | | 210.00 | `+55` |
| VAT deductible | 210.00 | | `-59` |
| Accounts payable | | 1,000.00 | — |

Net VAT cash effect: zero. But the return now shows base 1,000 in box 82, VAT due 210 in box 55, and VAT reclaimed 210 in box 59 — exactly what reverse charge requires. A **partially-deductible** tax would instead use two **positive** `tax` lines at 50% each (one to a VAT account `use_in_tax_closing=True`, one to an expense account `use_in_tax_closing=False`), splitting the 210 into 105 / 105.

### Retagging existing entries: `account_update_tax_tags`

When the law changes the tax report layout, already-posted entries keep their old grids. The optional module `account_update_tax_tags` ([__manifest__.py](../addons/account_update_tax_tags/__manifest__.py)) adds a debug-mode wizard that re-derives `tax_tag_ids` for existing move lines from the tax's *current* repartition lines. Base lines reach their tags by joining through the tax to the `base` repartition line, while tax lines already carry `tax_repartition_line_id` — see the SQL in [account_update_tax_tags_wizard.py:35](../addons/account_update_tax_tags/wizard/account_update_tax_tags_wizard.py#L35).

### Gotchas

- **Invoice and refund sets must mirror each other.** Same count, same order, same `factor_percent` per row, exactly one `base` line each, at least one `tax` line each — or `_validate_repartition_lines` raises ([account_tax.py:511](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L511)). You usually cannot add a `tax` line to only one direction.
- **`base` lines never carry an account.** Setting `repartition_type = base` clears `account_id` ([account_tax.py:5191-5194](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5191)); base lines only distribute *tags*, not money to a separate account.
- **Tag sign lives in the report formula, not the tag.** A `-59`-style negative box is encoded by the report expression's `-` prefix surfacing as `balance_negate` ([account_account_tag.py:65](../addons/account/models/account_account_tag.py#L65)), not by a sign field on the repartition line. The same physical tag can be read positive or negative depending on the report line.
- **Reverse-charge needs the explicit negative leg.** A `+100 / -100` pair must each total 100% on its own sign side ([account_tax.py:539-545](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L539)); a single 0%-net line will not produce the two grid entries the return expects.
- **`use_in_tax_closing` is auto-set from the account type.** A `tax` line pointing at an income/expense account is excluded from tax closing by default ([account_tax.py:5178](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5178)). If you book deductible VAT to the wrong account type, it silently drops out of the closing entry.
- **Cash-basis taxes substitute the account.** `_get_aml_target_tax_account` ([account_tax.py:5196](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5196)) returns the cash-basis *transition* account instead of `account_id` while the invoice is unpaid; tags for cash-basis taxes are only applied when `include_caba_tags` is set ([account_tax.py:2502-2504](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2502)).
- **Tags are countried.** A taxes-applicability tag belongs to a `country_id`, and the rep-line tag domain restricts to the company's fiscal country (plus foreign-VAT countries) — [account_tax.py:5172](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5172). You cannot freely attach a tag from another country's report.
- **Editing repartition lines on a used tax is logged, not silently applied retroactively.** Existing entries keep their tags until you run the `account_update_tax_tags` wizard; `repartition_lines_str` tracking ([account_tax.py:346](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L346)) records the change for audit.

## Reverse Charge (Intra-EU & Domestic)

**In plain terms:** under reverse charge, the *buyer* — not the seller — accounts for the VAT. The classic case is an intra-EU B2B purchase: your German supplier invoices you with no VAT, and you (the Belgian buyer) must both *declare* the VAT you owe and *reclaim* the same VAT, netting to zero cash but showing both figures on your return. Odoo models this with one tax that books two equal-and-opposite tax lines.

> **Decisions & risks (managers):** Reverse charge is not a rate you invent — it's a tax whose repartition is configured `+100% / −100%`, normally shipped by your country's localization and applied automatically through a fiscal position. The risk is *reporting*, not cash: if the two grids are wrong, your cash is still correct but the VAT return is misfiled. Always verify reverse-charge taxes come from the localization rather than hand-building them.

### How it works mechanically

A reverse-charge tax is an ordinary `account.tax` whose **tax** repartition lines carry both a positive and a negative leg: one `+100%` line to the VAT-payable account and one `−100%` line to the VAT-deductible account (see [Tax Distribution](#tax-distribution--tax-grids-repartition-lines--tags) for the full repartition model). Three engine facts make it behave:

- The validation sums positive-factor and negative-factor `tax` lines **independently**: the positives must total +100% and the negatives −100% ([account_tax.py:539-545](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L539)).
- `has_negative_factor` ([account_tax.py:199](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L199), computed at [account_tax.py:457](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L457)) flags a tax that has a negative leg; the engine then produces a paired, negated entry.
- A reverse-charge tax is **forced price-excluded** regardless of the company's price-include setting — and, since 20, regardless of the document's `document_tax_mode` too: `_is_price_included` returns `False` for any tax with `has_negative_factor` before looking at anything else ([account_tax.py:890-891](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L890)). The base grids are taken from the non-reverse-charge side ([account_tax.py:2431](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2431)); the `+` and `−` legs land on separate journal lines with their own tax grids ([account_tax.py:2435-2440](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2435)).

### Where it is configured

You rarely build this tax by hand. A localization ships the 0%/reverse-charge tax, and a **fiscal position** maps your domestic VAT to it (`original_tax_ids` / "Replaces", see [Fiscal Positions](#fiscal-positions-mapping-taxes--accounts-by-customer)). When an EU-B2B partner is detected, the line's domestic 21% is swapped for the reverse-charge tax automatically.

### Resulting journal entry (net 1,000 intra-EU purchase, 21%)

| Line | Debit | Credit | Tax grid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expense (base) | 1,000.00 | | base box (e.g. `+82`) |
| VAT payable (+100%) | | 210.00 | VAT-due box (e.g. `+55`) |
| VAT deductible (−100%) | 210.00 | | VAT-reclaimed box (e.g. `−59`) |
| Accounts payable | | 1,000.00 | — |

Net VAT cash effect is **zero**, but the return shows the base, the VAT due, and the VAT reclaimed — exactly what reverse charge requires. The same pattern serves domestic reverse-charge regimes (construction, scrap metal, electronics in some countries). For the full worked walkthrough see the [reverse-charge example](#worked-example-21-reverse-charge-purchase) in Tax Distribution.

## Tax Groups & Invoice Tax Totals

### Why tax groups exist

A single document can carry many different taxes (VAT 21%, VAT 6%, eco-tax, withholding). Showing one line per tax under the total would be noisy and would not match how accountants and tax authorities think. The **tax group** (`account.tax.group`) is the bucket that aggregates taxes for two purposes:

1. **Display** — on invoices, sales orders and purchase orders, taxes are summed *per group* and shown as one line each ("VAT 21%: 210.00").
2. **Tax reporting** — the group is the aggregation unit the tax report and the closing entry think in. **Changed in 20:** the group no longer *names* the closing GL accounts; those moved to the enterprise `account.return.type` ([account_return.py:173](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L173)).

Every `account.tax` points to exactly one group via `tax_group_id`. The group is the unit of summarization, not the individual tax.

### The `account.tax.group` model

Defined at [account_tax.py:25](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L25), ordered `sequence asc, id` — the order that drives the totals block. Its company scope is `_check_company_domain = check_company_domain_parent_of` ([account_tax.py:30](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L30)), so a **parent company's tax group is valid for its child/branch companies** — not strictly one company. (`_check_company_auto=True` is only the flag that triggers the check; the domain defines the rule. The same parent-of scoping applies to `account.tax` itself, [account_tax.py:63](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L63).)

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `name` | Translatable label shown on documents, e.g. "VAT 21%" — see [account_tax.py:32](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L32) |
| `sequence` | Display + sort order of the group inside a subtotal — [account_tax.py:33](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L33) |
| `company_id` | Owning company; groups are per-company — [account_tax.py:34](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L34) |
| `country_id` | Country the group applies to; auto-computed from the company's fiscal country — [account_tax.py:35](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L35) |
| `country_code` | Related shortcut on `country_id.code` — [account_tax.py:41](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L41) |
| `preceding_subtotal` | If set, defines a *named subtotal* shown before this group (multi-subtotal layout) — [account_tax.py:42](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L42) |
| `pos_receipt_label` | Short label for POS receipts (`group_label` in the JSON) — [account_tax.py:48](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L48) |

> **Changed in 20 — the closing accounts left this model.** In 19 the group also carried `tax_payable_account_id`, `tax_receivable_account_id` and `advance_tax_payment_account_id`. All three were **removed from community `account`** and now live on the enterprise `account.return.type` ([account_return.py:162-196](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L162)), which is what the tax closing reads. Code that wrote those fields on `account.tax.group` — including localization chart templates — had to move to the return type; the l10n `*_reports` modules now set them there (e.g. [template_it.py:12](../enterprise/l10n_it_reports/models/template_it.py#L12)). The community group is now purely a *display* object: `name`, `sequence`, `preceding_subtotal` and `pos_receipt_label` are all the totals widget consumes.

`country_id` is computed and stored but editable: `_compute_country_id` defaults it to `account_fiscal_country_id` (falling back to `country_id`) of the company — [account_tax.py:51](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L51).

### How totals are built: `_get_tax_totals_summary`

All three document types call one shared builder on `account.tax`: [`_get_tax_totals_summary`](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2746). It takes pre-computed base lines, a currency and a company, and returns the JSON dict the frontend renders. There is a parallel JS implementation in `account_tax.js` kept deliberately in sync (note the comment at [account_tax.py:2751-2752](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2751)) so the same totals can be computed live in the browser without a server round-trip.

The build runs in two aggregation passes:

1. **Global pass** — taxes are aggregated with a trivial grouping function (`True` if there is tax) to get the document-wide `base_amount` (untaxed) and `tax_amount` totals — [account_tax.py:2822-2832](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2822).
2. **Per-group pass** — the grouping function returns `tax_data['tax'].tax_group_id`, so base lines are re-aggregated by tax group — [account_tax.py:2845-2849](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2845). Groups are then sorted by `(sequence, id)` — [account_tax.py:2850-2855](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2850).

For each group the builder collects the involved tax ids, computes a **display base amount** (which can differ from the raw base for `fixed` and price-included `division` taxes — [account_tax.py:2871-2892](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2871)), then appends a group dict into a subtotal keyed by `preceding_subtotal` — [account_tax.py:2902](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2902).

#### Subtotals and the `preceding_subtotal` mechanism

A *subtotal* is a labeled running total in the totals block. By default every group lands under the implicit `"Untaxed Amount"` subtotal — [account_tax.py:2898](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2898). If a group sets `preceding_subtotal`, that string becomes a *new* subtotal heading, and the group is filed under it instead.

The key idea is **cascading bases**: each subtotal's `base_amount` is the untaxed amount *plus all tax accumulated so far*. The loop at [account_tax.py:2919-2930](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2919) keeps `accumulated_tax_amount` running and adds it into each subsequent subtotal's base — [account_tax.py:2924-2925](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2924). This is exactly what you need for tax-on-tax jurisdictions (e.g. a second tax computed on "net + first tax"): the second subtotal shows the higher base, and the group beneath it applies to that base.

`subtotals_order` preserves first-encounter order so subtotals render in the order their groups appear by sequence — [account_tax.py:2899-2900](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2899), [account_tax.py:2919](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2919).

#### The returned JSON shape

The full contract is documented inline at [account_tax.py:2762-2810](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2762). Top level:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `currency_id`, `currency_pd` | Document currency id and rounding (the JS widget uses `_pd` to format) |
| `company_currency_id`, `company_currency_pd` | Company currency equivalents |
| `has_tax_groups` | True if any tax group is involved; toggles the "separator" styling on the Total — [account_tax.py:2829](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2829) |
| `same_tax_base` | True when every group shares the same base, so the per-group base is redundant to show — [account_tax.py:2999](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2999) |
| `base_amount(_currency)` | Untaxed total |
| `tax_amount(_currency)` | Total tax |
| `total_amount(_currency)` | Grand total — [account_tax.py:3031](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3031) |

### Worked example: VAT 21% + VAT 6%

Invoice with two lines, two different tax groups, no preceding subtotal on either:

| Line | Net | Tax | Group |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1000.00 | VAT 21% | "VAT 21%" (seq 10) |
| B | 500.00 | VAT 6% | "VAT 6%" (seq 20) |

Global pass: `base_amount` = 1500.00, `tax_amount` = 210.00 + 30.00 = 240.00, `total_amount` = 1740.00.

Per-group pass produces two group dicts, sorted by sequence. Neither sets `preceding_subtotal`, so both land in the single `"Untaxed Amount"` subtotal. That subtotal's base is `base_amount + accumulated_tax_so_far` = 1500.00 (accumulated tax starts at 0 for the first/only subtotal).

Resulting JSON (abridged):

```
subtotals: [
  { name: "Untaxed Amount", base_amount_currency: 1500.00,
    tax_amount_currency: 240.00,
    tax_groups: [
      { group_name: "VAT 21%", base_amount_currency: 1000.00, tax_amount_currency: 210.00 },
      { group_name: "VAT 6%",  base_amount_currency:  500.00, tax_amount_currency:  30.00 },
    ] } ],
base_amount_currency: 1500.00, tax_amount_currency: 240.00, total_amount_currency: 1740.00,
has_tax_groups: true, same_tax_base: false
```

Rendered block:

```
Untaxed Amount        1,500.00
VAT 21%                 210.00
VAT 6%                   30.00
Total                 1,740.00
```

If instead "VAT 6%" had `preceding_subtotal = "Subtotal with 21% VAT"`, a second subtotal would appear whose base is `1500.00 + 210.00 = 1710.00` (the accumulated 21% tax rolled into the base), and the 6% group would sit beneath it — the multi-subtotal layout used for tax-on-tax.

### Gotchas

- **`tax_totals` is `None` on non-invoice moves.** Journal entries, bank statements etc. never populate it; the widget only works where every line shares one currency — [account_move.py:1937-1939](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1937).
- **`display_base_amount` ≠ `base_amount`.** For `fixed` taxes the display base is `False` (hidden), and for price-included `division` taxes it is recomputed — do not assume the shown base equals the raw base — [account_tax.py:2871-2892](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2871).
- **`same_tax_base` controls base visibility.** When all groups share one base the per-group base is treated as redundant; localizations/printouts use this flag to suppress repeating it — [account_tax.py:2999](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2999).
- **Subtotal bases cascade.** A second subtotal's base already includes the tax of preceding groups. Reading a `preceding_subtotal` base as "net only" is wrong — it is net + accumulated tax — [account_tax.py:2924-2925](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2924).
- **Group `sequence` (not tax sequence) orders the totals.** Re-ordering taxes does nothing to the totals block; you must reorder the `account.tax.group` records — [account_tax.py:2850-2855](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2850).
- **Two parallel implementations.** The Python builder has a JS twin in `account_tax.js`; changing one without the other silently desyncs server PDF totals from the live on-screen totals — [account_tax.py:2751-2752](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2751).
- **Cash-rounding & non-deductible adjustments mutate group/subtotal amounts after the main loop.** `biggest_tax` rounding loads the delta onto the largest tax group, and non-deductible handling (Python-only, not in JS) subtracts amounts back out — totals can shift in these later stages — [account_tax.py:2965-2984](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2965), [account_tax.py:2999](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2999).
- **The widget's group amount is editable.** Manual edits write back through `_inverse_tax_totals` — [tax_totals.js:304](../addons/account/static/src/components/tax_totals/tax_totals.js#L304), [account_move.py:2676](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L2676). (In 19 this gotcha also warned that the group's closing accounts are unrelated to the edit path; in 20 the group has no accounts at all.)

## Fiscal Positions: Mapping Taxes & Accounts by Customer

A **fiscal position** is a rule set that adapts a document's taxes and ledger accounts to *who* you are trading with. The same product line should carry domestic VAT for a local customer, 0% reverse-charge for an EU business, and no tax at all for an export outside the bloc. Rather than maintaining three product variants, you define one product with default (domestic) taxes and let the fiscal position rewrite them per partner.

The model lives — somewhat unexpectedly — in [partner.py:33](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L33), because fiscal positions are conceptually a property of the partner relationship.

### The two mappings

A fiscal position holds two independent mapping tables.

| Mapping | Field | What it swaps |
|---|---|---|
| Tax mapping | `tax_ids` (M2M to `account.tax`) | a *source* tax to its *destination* tax(es), or removes it |
| Account mapping | `account_ids` (O2M to `account.fiscal.position.account`) | a product's default income/expense account to another account |

The tax mapping is defined at [partner.py:50](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L50). Note the data model: `tax_ids` is just the flat list of *destination* taxes. The source→destination link is stored on the tax itself, via `account.tax.original_tax_ids` ("Replaces"), defined at [account_tax.py:87](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L87). At runtime `_compute_tax_map` inverts this into a dict `{src_tax_id: [dest_tax_id, ...]}` at [partner.py:106](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L106). **Changed in 20:** `tax_map` and `account_map` are `fields.Json` instead of `fields.Binary` ([partner.py:49](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L49), [partner.py:57](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L57)) — same content, but now readable from SQL and transported as JSON.

> **Read the mapping table carefully:** `tax_ids` holds only the *destination* taxes. You don't type a "from → to" pair here — you set the **destination** tax's **Replaces** field (`original_tax_ids`) to point at the domestic tax it stands in for.

**What makes a tax replaceable — `is_domestic`.** A destination tax can only replace a tax that is *domestic*. `account.tax.is_domestic` is `True` when the tax has no fiscal position, or when the company's **domestic** fiscal position is among its `fiscal_position_ids` ([account_tax.py:109](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L109), computed at [account_tax.py:319](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L319)) — this is exactly the **"Fiscal Position: Domestic"** value on the tax form header. `original_tax_ids` is domain-restricted to `is_domestic=True` taxes of the matching `type_tax_use`, and its reverse field is `replacing_tax_ids` ("Replaced by") ([account_tax.py:100](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L100)). Two consequences for setup: you cannot point a replacement at a non-domestic or wrong-direction tax, and a mis-set company `domestic_fiscal_position_id` ([company.py:206](../addons/account/models/company.py#L206), computed at [company.py:437](../addons/account/models/company.py#L437)) silently changes which taxes count as domestic.

The account mapping uses the intermediate model `account.fiscal.position.account` ([partner.py:323](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L323)), which has just `account_src_id` ("Account on Product") and `account_dest_id` ("Account to Use Instead"). `_compute_account_map` flattens it into `{src: dest}` at [partner.py:115](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L115).

### How a mapping is applied

`map_tax(taxes)` ([partner.py:161](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L161)) is the workhorse. For each incoming tax it looks up `tax_map.get(tax.id, [tax.id])` — so a tax with no mapping passes through unchanged, and a mapped tax is replaced by its destination list. Two edge behaviors matter:

- Calling `map_tax` on an **empty recordset** (no fiscal position) returns the taxes untouched ([partner.py:162-163](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L162)).
- A tax that is not in `tax_map` survives only if it is domestic for this position — i.e. if `self in tax.fiscal_position_ids` or the tax has no `fiscal_position_ids` at all; otherwise it is dropped ([partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170)). So a fiscal position whose `tax_ids` is empty (e.g. one auto-created for a tax unit) **removes every tax that names a fiscal position**.

`map_account(account)` ([partner.py:177](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L177)) is the simpler sibling: one account in, the mapped account (or the original) out.

These get called wherever default taxes/accounts are resolved:

| Caller | Where |
|---|---|
| Invoice line tax computation | [account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336) — after taking product/account default taxes |
| Invoice line account mapping | [account_move_line.py:777](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L777) |
| Sale order line | [sale_order_line.py:727](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L727) |
| Purchase order line | [purchase_order_line.py:197](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L197) |

On an invoice line, `_get_computed_taxes` ([account_move_line.py:1302](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1302)) first picks the product's `taxes_id` (sales) or `supplier_taxes_id` (purchases), then, only if `move.fiscal_position_id` is set, runs them through `map_tax`. So the product always defines the *domestic baseline*; the fiscal position is a post-processor.

### Price-included re-pricing (`_adapt_price_unit_to_another_taxes`)

A subtle B2C case: when a fiscal position maps one **tax-included** tax to another tax-included tax with a *different rate* — say a 6%-included shelf price for one country to a 21%-included rate for another — the gross price itself must move, so that 106 becomes 121 and the *net* stays 100. `_adapt_price_unit_to_another_taxes` ([account_tax.py:1320](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1320)) does this, invoked from `product._get_tax_included_unit_price_from_price` ([product.py:420](../addons/account/models/product.py#L420), itself called by `_get_tax_included_unit_price` at [product.py:356](../addons/account/models/product.py#L356)). It **short-circuits** (leaves the price untouched) if the original and new taxes are identical, or if *any* original tax is price-**excluded** — re-pricing happens only included → included.

> **Decisions & risks (managers):** This is why two B2C webshops in different VAT countries can show different gross prices for the same net product, while a B2B (tax-excluded) catalogue keeps one net price everywhere. If your prices are tax-excluded, fiscal positions never touch the displayed unit price — only the tax line changes.

### Auto-detection: how a partner gets its position

A user can pin a position manually on the contact via `property_account_position_id` ([partner.py:593](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L593)) — a `company_dependent` field, so the same partner can have different positions per company. Otherwise Odoo detects one automatically.

`_get_fiscal_position(partner, delivery)` ([partner.py:259](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L259)) is the entry point, called from the move's `_compute_fiscal_position_id` ([account_move.py:1080](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1080)) with the delivery partner resolved from `partner_shipping_id`. Its logic, in order:

1. Compute `intra_eu` and `vat_exclusion` from the VAT prefixes of company and partner ([partner.py:269](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L269)). If both are in the EU **and share the same prefix** (same country), detection falls back to the invoicing partner instead of the delivery address.
2. A **manually set** `property_account_position_id` always wins ([partner.py:279-284](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L279)).
3. If the partner has no country, return nothing.
4. Otherwise search all positions with `auto_apply = True` ([partner.py:289](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L289)) and return the first match.

`auto_apply` ([partner.py:59](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L59)) is the master switch that makes a position eligible for detection. The match itself runs through `_get_first_matching_fpos` ([partner.py:220](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L220)), which sorts **company-specific positions first, then by `sequence`**, and returns the first whose every validation function passes. Those functions live in `_get_fpos_validation_functions` ([partner.py:227](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L227)):

| Criterion | Field(s) | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| VAT required | `vat_required` | partner must pass `_get_vat_required_valid` — by default just "has a VAT number" ([partner.py:924](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L924)). **Changed in 20:** `base_vat` no longer exists; VAT format checking moved into `account` and the **VIES** online check now lives in the new `l10n_eu_account_vies` module, which overrides `_get_vat_required_valid` to demand a VIES-valid number ([l10n_eu_account_vies/res_partner.py:178](../addons/l10n_eu_account_vies/models/res_partner.py#L178)) |
| Zip range | `zip_from` / `zip_to` | partner zip falls within the range |
| State | `state_ids` | partner state is in the list |
| Country | `country_id` | partner country matches exactly |
| Country group | `country_group_id` | partner country is in the group, and its state is not excluded |

Each criterion is *skip-if-empty*: an unset field never blocks a match. So a position with only `country_group_id = Europe` and `vat_required = True` matches any EU partner that has a VAT number.

### foreign_vat: registrations abroad

> **Decisions & risks (managers):** Each foreign VAT registration is its **own** fiscal position carrying its own `foreign_vat` and country. Treat these as setup constraints, not footnotes: **one foreign-VAT position per country**, and the position drives the *tax-report country* for the documents that use it. Selling into several countries where you are VAT-registered means one such position each.

`foreign_vat` ([partner.py:73](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L73)) holds your company's tax ID in *another* region — the backbone of EU OSS and any foreign VAT registration. When set, the position represents "we are registered to collect tax in country X." A constraint (`_validate_foreign_vat_country`, [partner.py:126](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L126)) enforces that such a position has a `country_id`, forbids two foreign-VAT positions for the same country, and — if the foreign VAT is *within your own fiscal country* — requires a `state_ids` (think US states). On a posted move, the foreign VAT position drives `tax_country_id` for reporting ([account_move.py:783](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L783), computed at [account_move.py:2031](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L2031)). `action_create_foreign_taxes` ([partner.py:310](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L310)) can even install the localization and instantiate that country's taxes for you.

### Worked example 1 — EU B2B reverse charge (BE 21% to 0%)

A Belgian company sells consulting to a German business customer (`DE` VAT). Domestic product tax is **21% VAT**.

Setup:
- Position "Intra-EU B2B": `auto_apply = True`, `country_group_id = Europe`, `vat_required = True`.
- A 0% "Reverse charge" tax whose `original_tax_ids` = [21% domestic], so `tax_map = {21%_id: [0%_RC_id]}`.

Flow on a 1,000 EUR invoice line:
1. Partner is German with a VAT number, in Europe, prefix `DE` ≠ company `BE` → no same-prefix fallback. The Intra-EU B2B position auto-applies ([partner.py:289](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L289)).
2. Line picks the product default **21%**. Because a position is set, `map_tax([21%])` runs ([account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336)).
3. `tax_map[21%_id]` → `[0%_RC_id]`, so the line ends with the **0% reverse-charge tax**.

Result: 1,000 EUR invoiced, 0 VAT charged, with the reverse-charge tax recording the notional amounts on both sides for the VAT return.

### Worked example 2 — Tax-exempt export

The same Belgian company ships goods to a customer in the **United States**.

Setup:
- Position "Export (extra-EU)": `auto_apply = True`, `country_id = United States` (or a non-EU country group). Its `tax_ids` contains a 0% "Export" tax mapping `21% → 0% export`. Mapping `21% → ∅` (no destination) would instead strip the tax entirely.

Flow:
1. US partner, country matches → Export position auto-applies.
2. `map_tax([21%])` returns the **0% export tax** (or empty, if you mapped to nothing).

Result: no domestic VAT on the export, and (if you also configured `account_ids`) sales can post to a dedicated export-revenue account via `map_account`.

### Gotchas

- **The source→destination link lives on the tax, not the position.** You add the *destination* tax to `tax_ids`, but you must set that destination tax's `original_tax_ids` ("Replaces") to point at the domestic tax. Forgetting this leaves `tax_map` empty and `map_tax` silently passes domestic taxes through ([partner.py:161](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L161)).
- **Empty `tax_ids` deletes every tax that names a fiscal position**, it does not mean "no change" ([partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170)). This is intentional for tax-unit positions but a trap if you create an empty position by accident.
- **A manual `property_account_position_id` overrides every auto-apply rule** ([partner.py:279-284](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L279)) — a stale value on a contact silently defeats your detection logic.
- **Detection uses the delivery partner, not the invoicing partner** ([account_move.py:1088](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1088)) — except the same-EU-prefix case, which flips back to invoicing ([partner.py:275-276](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L275)). A vendor **receipt** (`in_receipt`) short-circuits all of it and takes the company's `account_purchase_receipt_fiscal_position_id` ([account_move.py:1082](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1082)). **Outcome:** when your company and the partner share the same EU VAT prefix (same country), the partner is treated as **domestic** and charged domestic VAT — relevant for same-country multi-branch setups. Place-of-supply surprises usually trace to a wrong shipping address.
- **No country on the partner means no auto-detection** at all ([partner.py:286](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L286)); manually pinned positions still apply.
- **Match order is company-specificity then `sequence`** ([partner.py:221](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L221)), and the *first* match wins. Overlapping positions (e.g. a country position and a country-group position) need careful sequencing.
- **Skip-if-empty criteria** mean a position with no filters and `auto_apply = True` matches *everyone* — easy to create accidentally and hard to notice.
- **`auto_apply = False` positions are invisible to detection** but can still be assigned manually; they never appear in the auto-apply search ([partner.py:289](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L289)).
- **The zip-range constraint** requires both `zip_from` and `zip_to`, with `to >= from` ([partner.py:120](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L120)); zip comparison is left-padded string comparison via `_convert_zip_values` ([partner.py:194](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L194)), not numeric.
- **One foreign-VAT position per country** is enforced by constraint ([partner.py:126](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L126)).

## Taxes on Products & Company Defaults

When you add a product to a customer invoice, sales order, vendor bill, or purchase order, Odoo fills in the tax column automatically. That value does not come from nowhere — it walks a precedence chain that starts on the product, falls back to the account, gets filtered by company, and is finally remapped by the customer's fiscal position. This section traces that chain end to end and shows where the very first default (the company-wide tax applied to brand-new products) lives.

### The two tax fields on a product

A product carries two separate default-tax lists, one per direction of trade:

| Field | Label in UI | Used when | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| `taxes_id` | Sales Taxes / Customer Taxes | selling: sales orders, customer invoices | `type_tax_use = 'sale'` |
| `supplier_taxes_id` | Purchase Taxes / Vendor Taxes | buying: purchase orders, vendor bills | `type_tax_use = 'purchase'` |

Both are `Many2many` to `account.tax` and both are defined on `product.template`, so they are shared by all variants of a product. See [product.py:79](../addons/account/models/product.py#L79) for `taxes_id` and [product.py:86](../addons/account/models/product.py#L86) for `supplier_taxes_id`. The `domain` on each field is what keeps a purchase tax out of the customer-tax picker and vice versa.

These are the *defaults the product proposes*. They are not necessarily the taxes that end up on a posted line — see the precedence chain below.

### Where a new product gets its tax: the company default

The interesting part is the `default=` on each field. A freshly created product does not start empty; it inherits the company's configured default tax:

```python
default=lambda self: self.env.companies.account_sale_tax_id
                     or self.env.companies.root_id.sudo().account_sale_tax_id
```

That lambda ([product.py:83](../addons/account/models/product.py#L83) for sales, [product.py:90](../addons/account/models/product.py#L90) for purchases) reads `account_sale_tax_id` / `account_purchase_tax_id` off the company. If the active company has no default set, it climbs to the root company and reads it there with `sudo()` (the user may not have access to the parent company's record).

Those two fields live on `res.company`:

- `account_sale_tax_id` — "Default Sale Tax" — [company.py:129](../addons/account/models/company.py#L129)
- `account_purchase_tax_id` — "Default Purchase Tax" — [company.py:130](../addons/account/models/company.py#L130)

> **New in 20 — `vat_disabled`.** A company can be flagged **Not Subject to VAT** ([company.py:226](../addons/account/models/company.py#L226), exposed in Settings via [res_config_settings.py:60](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L60)). The option is offered only to EU companies and Switzerland ([company.py:491](../addons/account/models/company.py#L491)). Turning it on archives the normal sales taxes, activates a localization-supplied no-VAT tax and points `account_sale_tax_id` at it ([company.py:528-532](../addons/account/models/company.py#L528)); invoice lines then short-circuit the whole precedence chain and take `_get_default_taxes_on_vat_disabled()` ([account_move_line.py:1340](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1340)).

You set them in **Accounting → Configuration → Settings → Taxes → Default Taxes**. The settings form does not write the company fields directly; it exposes two `related` proxy fields, `sale_tax_id` and `purchase_tax_id`, that write straight through to the company. See [res_config_settings.py:38](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L38) and [res_config_settings.py:46](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L46), and the view block titled "Default Taxes" at [res_config_settings_views.xml:64](../addons/account/views/res_config_settings_views.xml#L64). The help text there is explicit: "Default taxes applied when creating new products."

So the data flow for the default is: **Settings form → `res.config.settings.sale_tax_id` (related) → `res.company.account_sale_tax_id` → product `taxes_id` default at create time.**

### Multi-company: products with no company

A product can be company-specific (`company_id` set) or shared across all companies (`company_id` empty). A shared product must hold sensible defaults for *every* company, not just the one you happened to be in when you created it. The `create` override handles this: after creating products, it searches for all companies outside the current allowed set, and for any product without a `company_id` it force-adds those other companies' default taxes too. See the `create` override at [product.py:295-306](../addons/account/models/product.py#L295), which delegates to `_force_default_tax` → `_force_default_sale_tax` / `_force_default_purchase_tax` at [product.py:291](../addons/account/models/product.py#L291), [product.py:271](../addons/account/models/product.py#L271), [product.py:281](../addons/account/models/product.py#L281). It runs `sudo()` because the user may not be allowed to read the other companies' tax configuration.

The consequence: a shared product can legitimately carry several taxes in `taxes_id` — one per company. That is why the rest of the system never trusts `taxes_id` blindly; it always filters by the relevant company first.

`_filter_taxes_by_company` is the helper that does this, at [account_tax.py:4967](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4967). **Changed in 20:** its parameter went from `company_id` to `company`. It keeps only the taxes whose `company_id` matches the target company, and if none match it walks *up* the company hierarchy (`company.parent_id`) until it finds some. This gives child companies the parent's taxes when they have none of their own.

### The precedence chain on a real line

When a product hits a move line, `_compute_tax_ids` recomputes the tax column, calling `_get_computed_taxes` ([account_move_line.py:1302](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1302)). The resolution order is:

1. **Product tax, filtered to the line's company.** On a sale document it reads `product_id.taxes_id`; on a purchase document `product_id.supplier_taxes_id`. Both are immediately narrowed with a company domain — [account_move_line.py:1312](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1312) and [account_move_line.py:1321](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1321). (A `vat_disabled` company skips straight past all of this on sales documents — [account_move_line.py:1305](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1305).)
2. **Fallback to the account's taxes.** If the product proposes no matching tax, it falls back to the taxes configured on the line's account (`account_id.tax_ids`), keeping only those of the right `type_tax_use` — the `or` on those same lines.
3. **Filter by company again.** `tax_ids._filter_taxes_by_company(self.company_id)` at [account_move_line.py:1333](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1333).
4. **Fiscal position remap.** If the move has a `fiscal_position_id`, the resolved taxes are run through `map_tax` — [account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336).

`map_tax` ([partner.py:161](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L161)) looks each source tax up in the position's `tax_map` and substitutes the destination tax(es); unmapped taxes pass through unchanged. A special case: an *empty* fiscal position (one with no `tax_ids` at all, like those created by tax units) strips every tax that names a fiscal position — [partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170). The `tax_map` itself is a computed `{source_tax_id: [dest_tax_id, ...]}` dictionary built from the position's mapping lines — [partner.py:106](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L106).

So the full chain is: **product tax → (else account tax) → company filter → fiscal-position mapping → line tax.**

### The `tax_string` price hint

On the product form, just under the sales price, Odoo shows a small grey note like `(= $121.00 Incl. Taxes)`. That is `tax_string`, a computed `Char` — [product.py:85](../addons/account/models/product.py#L85), computed by `_compute_tax_string` at [product.py:221](../addons/account/models/product.py#L221), which delegates to `_construct_tax_string` at [product.py:225](../addons/account/models/product.py#L225).

Its purpose is to tell the user, at a glance, what the entered list price means once tax is applied. The logic:

- It computes `compute_all(price)` using the product's taxes, but only the ones valid for the current company (`_filter_taxes_by_company(self.env.company)` — [product.py:227](../addons/account/models/product.py#L227)).
- If `total_included` differs from the typed price, it appends `"X Incl. Taxes"`.
- If `total_excluded` differs, it appends `"X Excl. Taxes"`.
- If the price already equals both (no tax, or a 0% tax), the string is blank.

This is purely informational — it never changes the stored price. It is `@api.depends_context('company')` so a multi-company user sees the note recomputed for whichever company they are in.

### Worked example

**Setup.** Company "GEC Ltd" has Default Sale Tax = `21%` (price-excluded). Set under Settings → Taxes → Default Taxes.

1. **Create a product**, list price `100.00`. Because of the `default=` lambda at [product.py:83](../addons/account/models/product.py#L83), `taxes_id` is pre-filled with the `21%` tax. The form shows `tax_string` = `(= $121.00 Incl. Taxes)`: `total_included` (121) differs from the typed 100, so the "Incl." branch fires at [product.py:232-233](../addons/account/models/product.py#L232).

2. **Add it to a customer invoice with no fiscal position.** `_get_computed_taxes` ([account_move_line.py:1302](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1302)) takes the product's `21%`, confirms it belongs to GEC Ltd via the company filter, finds no fiscal position, and stops. Line tax = `21%`. Invoice line: base 100, tax 21, total 121.

3. **Now the customer is in the EU and assigned a fiscal position** "Intra-EU B2B" that maps `21%` → `0% EU` (reverse charge). Same product, same line. Steps 1–3 of the chain still resolve to `21%`, but step 4 kicks in: `fiscal_position_id.map_tax([21%])` ([account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336)) returns the `tax_map` substitution `[0% EU]`. Line tax = `0% EU`. Invoice line: base 100, tax 0, total 100.

The product configuration never changed. The fiscal position rewrote the tax at the moment the line was computed.

### Gotchas

- **The product's tax is a default, not a verdict.** `taxes_id` proposes; the fiscal position can override on each document. Auditing a posted line against the product's tax field can mislead you — read the line's actual `tax_ids`.
- **Shared products hold multiple taxes.** A product with empty `company_id` accumulates one default tax per company (via the `create` override at [product.py:295-306](../addons/account/models/product.py#L295)). `taxes_id` showing several taxes is normal, not a bug; each line filters down to its own company.
- **No company default = empty product tax.** If `account_sale_tax_id` is unset on both the company and its root, new products are created with no sales tax and you will silently invoice at 0% until someone notices.
- **The fallback is the *account's* taxes, not "no tax".** If a product has no matching company tax, the line borrows `account_id.tax_ids` ([account_move_line.py:1313](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1313)). A surprise tax on a line can come from the income/expense account, not the product.
- **An empty fiscal position deletes taxes.** A fiscal position with no mapping lines is treated as "remove everything that names a fiscal position," not "change nothing" — [partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170). This is intentional for tax units but trips people up.
- **`tax_string` is company-aware and price-aware only.** It ignores fiscal positions entirely (it passes an empty partner at [product.py:227-229](../addons/account/models/product.py#L227)), so the note on the product form can differ from the tax a specific EU customer actually pays.
- **Combo products force-clear both tax fields** on type change — [product.py:265-269](../addons/account/models/product.py#L265) — because tax lives on the combo's components, not the combo itself.
- **Settings writes go through `related` proxies.** Editing the default tax in Settings actually writes `res.company.account_sale_tax_id`; there is no separate config record storing it ([res_config_settings.py:38](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L38)).

## Taxes in Sales (Quotation, Sales Order, Invoice)

**In plain terms:** a quotation, a confirmed sales order, and a customer invoice are three states of the *same* document family. The sales order is the *commercial* document (it computes tax for display and for the customer's expected total); the invoice is the *accounting* document, where tax actually posts to the ledger. **In normal invoicing the invoice reproduces the order's taxes exactly** — the order line copies its `tax_ids` (and a pinned `extra_tax_data` blob) to the invoice line, and the invoice's own tax compute will not wipe an explicit tax set. The two diverge only in a few cases, chiefly when you *change the product or the fiscal position on the draft invoice*. Knowing when re-derivation does and does not fire is the key to this flow.

### Where a line's taxes come from: the product, the company, the fiscal position

A sale order line stores its taxes in `tax_ids`, a computed-but-editable field. Its compute is `_compute_tax_ids` ([sale_order_line.py:702](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L702)), triggered by `product_id` and `company_id`. The chain has three steps, in order:

1. **Start from the product's customer taxes.** Every product carries `taxes_id` — its default *Customer Taxes* ([product.py:79](../addons/account/models/product.py#L79)). The line reads them and immediately filters to the line's company: `line.product_id.taxes_id._filter_taxes_by_company()`, evaluated under `with_company(company)` ([sale_order_line.py:711-713](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L711)). This matters in multi-company databases — a product may carry taxes belonging to several companies, and only the order's company's taxes are relevant.
2. **Map through the order's fiscal position.** The filtered taxes are passed to `fiscal_position.map_tax(taxes)` ([sale_order_line.py:727](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L727)), memoised per `(fiscal position, company, taxes)` key so a long order maps once per distinct combination ([sale_order_line.py:721-728](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L721)). The fiscal position comes from the *order*, not the line: `line.order_id.fiscal_position_id`.
3. **Assign the result** to `line.tax_ids`. No fiscal position means `map_tax` returns the taxes unchanged.

**Changed in 20:** a **productless** line (a manually typed description with no product and no `display_type`) no longer ends up untaxed — it now falls back to the company's `account_sale_tax_id` ([sale_order_line.py:716-717](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L716)). Only a line that still resolves to no tax after that has `tax_ids` cleared ([sale_order_line.py:718-720](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L718)). Combo products clear taxes outright — tax lives on their component lines instead ([sale_order_line.py:706-708](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L706)).

Because the field is `readonly=False`, a salesperson can override taxes per line by hand. The compute only re-fires when `product_id` or `company_id` changes, so a manual override survives later edits to price or quantity. **New in 20:** when the recompute runs under the `recompute_unit_price_on_tax_change` context and the tax actually changed, the line's `price_unit` is queued for recomputation too ([sale_order_line.py:730-734](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L730)) — this is what keeps a tax-included shelf price honest when a fiscal position swaps the rate.

#### What `map_tax` actually does

The fiscal position is a substitution table. `map_tax` ([partner.py:161](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L161)) looks each input tax up in `tax_map` and replaces it with the configured target tax(es), or keeps it if there is no rule. Two edge cases are worth knowing: an empty recordset (no fiscal position) returns the taxes unchanged ([partner.py:162-163](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L162)); a fiscal position that *exists but has no tax mappings* — like the ones tax units auto-create — strips every tax that names a fiscal position ([partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170)). This is the mechanism behind "0% / exempt for foreign customers": the fiscal position maps the domestic 21% tax to an exempt tax, or to nothing.

### How the order resolves its fiscal position

The order's `fiscal_position_id` is computed by `_compute_fiscal_position_id` ([sale_order.py:609](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L609)), depending on `partner_shipping_id`, `partner_id`, and `company_id`. It delegates to `account.fiscal.position._get_fiscal_position(partner, delivery)` ([partner.py:259](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L259)), run `with_company` so the right company's rules apply. **New in 20:** the compute memoises the lookup per `(company, partner, shipping)` key ([sale_order.py:610-625](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L610)). The resolution priority:

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | A fiscal position **manually set** on the delivery or commercial partner (`property_account_position_id`) always wins ([partner.py:279-284](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L279)) |
| 2 | Otherwise, auto-detection by the delivery address's country / state / ZIP |
| 3 | EU intra-community VAT rules can redirect detection to the invoicing partner ([partner.py:275-276](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L275)) |

> **Changed in 20 — no more `show_update_fpos` on the sale order.** In 19 a changed fiscal position on an order with lines set a `show_update_fpos` flag that surfaced an "update taxes" banner. That field was **removed from `sale.order`** (it still exists on `account.move`, [account_move.py:800](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L800)). On the order the equivalent is now the explicit `_recompute_taxes()` helper, which re-runs `_compute_tax_ids` on every non-display line with `recompute_unit_price_on_tax_change=True` ([sale_order.py:1808-1811](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L1808)).

### Order totals: `amount_untaxed`, `amount_tax`, `amount_total`, and the `tax_totals` widget

These three monetary fields are computed together in `_compute_amounts` ([sale_order.py:726](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L726)). Rather than summing line subtotals naively, the order delegates the whole calculation to the generic `account.tax` engine:

1. Each priced line is converted to a "base line" dict via `_prepare_base_line_for_taxes_computation` ([sale_order_line.py:1024](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L1024)), which packages `tax_ids`, quantity, partner, currency, and rate.
2. `_add_tax_details_in_base_lines` and `_round_base_lines_tax_details` enrich and round them ([sale_order.py:730-737](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L730)).
3. `_get_tax_totals_summary` returns a dict; the order reads `base_amount_currency`, `tax_amount_currency`, `total_amount_currency` into the three fields ([sale_order.py:739](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L739)).

The on-screen breakdown ("Untaxed Amount / Tax 21% / Total") is a *separate* field, `tax_totals` ([sale_order.py:482](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L482)), computed by `_compute_tax_totals` ([sale_order.py:1178](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L1178)) using the *same* base-line pipeline. It is a `Json` blob rendered by the tax-totals OWL widget (**changed in 20**: the field was `Binary` in 19). It is recomputed live (it `@api.depends_context('lang')`) so the labels translate. Doing the totals through the shared engine — instead of per-line addition — is what makes price-included taxes, multi-tax lines, rounding-per-line vs per-tax, and early-payment-discount scenarios all come out consistent with the eventual invoice.

### Carrying taxes from order to invoice

When you invoice, each order line builds an invoice-line value dict in `_prepare_invoice_line` ([sale_order_line.py:2011](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L2011)). The taxes are copied straight across — along with `extra_tax_data`, a `Json` blob that pins the exact computed amounts (for down payments, global discounts, combos) so the invoice can't drift from the order ([sale_order_line.py:393](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L393)):

```python
"tax_ids": [Command.set(self.tax_ids.ids)],   # sale_order_line.py:2046
"extra_tax_data": self.extra_tax_data,        # sale_order_line.py:2049
```

The invoice header itself copies the order's fiscal position in `_prepare_invoice` ([sale_order.py:1836](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L1836)), falling back to resolving one from the invoicing partner if the order had none.

Here is the subtle part. The invoice line model (`account.move.line`) has its *own* tax compute, `_compute_tax_ids` ([account_move_line.py:1292](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1292)), depending on `product_id`. When the invoice is created with an explicit `tax_ids` from the order line, that compute does **not** wipe it — note the guard "Don't remove existing taxes if there is no explicit taxes set" ([account_move_line.py:1297](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1297)). But if the product changes on the invoice, `_get_computed_taxes` ([account_move_line.py:1302](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1302)) re-derives taxes from scratch using the **invoice's** context: product customer taxes filtered by company, then mapped through `move_id.fiscal_position_id` ([account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336)). So the order's taxes carry over as-is and are re-derived only when the **product** or the move's **fiscal position** changes on the draft invoice. The invoice remains the *posting* authority — it owns the tax lines and posting accounts — but in the normal flow those reproduce the order's taxes exactly. The only legitimate ways a draft invoice ends up with *different* taxes are: you change the product, you change the fiscal position, or you edit the taxes by hand.

The link back is preserved through `sale_line_ids` on the invoice line ([sale/account_move_line.py:12](../addons/sale/models/account_move_line.py#L12)), which is how invoiced quantities flow back to the order.

### Down payments

Down-payment invoices do not tax a normal product line; they tax a *slice* of the order's existing taxed amounts. The tax engine prepares this via `_prepare_base_lines_for_down_payment` ([account_tax.py:3872](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3872)), which folds every amount/tax that is *not* eligible for a down payment into the base, leaving only the discountable taxes to apply to the down-payment slice. `_prepare_down_payment_lines` ([account_tax.py:3893](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3893)) then reduces those base lines to the target percentage or fixed amount. The down-payment SO line is flagged `is_downpayment`, and its base-line dict carries `special_type='down_payment'` ([sale_order_line.py:1045](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L1045)) so the engine treats it correctly. The practical effect: a 30% down payment on a 21%-taxed order invoices 30% of the net *and* 30% of the tax, and the final invoice nets the down payment out so the customer is never double-taxed.

### Worked example

Quotation for company in a 21% VAT country, domestic customer (no fiscal position mapping):

- One line: product "Consulting", qty 10, unit price 100.00, tax = the product's customer tax **21%**.

Line subtotal (untaxed) = 10 × 100.00 = **1,000.00**. The order's `_compute_amounts` runs the base-line pipeline:

| Field | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `amount_untaxed` | 1,000.00 | `base_amount_currency` ([sale_order.py:739](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L739)) |
| `amount_tax` | 210.00 | `tax_amount_currency` ([sale_order.py:740](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L740)) |
| `amount_total` | 1,210.00 | `total_amount_currency` ([sale_order.py:741](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L741)) |

The `tax_totals` widget shows: Untaxed 1,000.00 / VAT 21% 210.00 / Total 1,210.00.

Confirm, then invoice. `_prepare_invoice_line` produces an invoice line with `product_id=Consulting, quantity=10, price_unit=100.00, tax_ids=[21%]` ([sale_order_line.py:2011](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L2011)). The invoice header gets the same (empty) fiscal position. The customer invoice independently computes the same 1,000.00 / 210.00 / 1,210.00 and, critically, posts a 210.00 tax journal line to the VAT-payable account — something the quotation never did. If a different fiscal position had been set on the customer (say, "Exempt"), the invoice's own `map_tax` would have stripped the 21% and the tax line would be 0.00, regardless of the 21% shown earlier.

### Gotchas

- **`tax_ids` only recomputes on product/company change.** Changing price, quantity, or even the fiscal position after the line exists does **not** auto-remap line taxes — the compute depends on `product_id` and `company_id` only ([sale_order_line.py:701](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L701)). **Changed in 20:** the `show_update_fpos` prompt is gone from the sale order; call `_recompute_taxes()` ([sale_order.py:1808-1811](../addons/sale/models/sale_order.py#L1808)) — or the "Update Taxes" server action that wraps it — instead. A line whose taxes were edited by hand keeps them.
- **An "empty" fiscal position is not the same as no fiscal position.** A fiscal position with no tax mappings strips every tax that names a fiscal position ([partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170)); a `False` fiscal position leaves taxes untouched. Tax units create the former kind silently.
- **Fiscal position follows the delivery address, not the invoice address.** Auto-detection keys on shipping country/state/ZIP, except where EU intra-community VAT rules redirect it ([partner.py:275-276](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L275)).
- **The invoice can legitimately show different taxes than the order.** Order taxes are only a seed; the invoice line recomputes from the product and the invoice's own fiscal position whenever the product changes ([account_move_line.py:1336](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1336)). The invoice is the source of truth for posting.
- **Order totals are computed by the tax engine, not by summing line subtotals.** Two near-identical fields exist: `amount_tax` (a stored Monetary for reporting) and `tax_totals` (a non-stored JSON blob for the UI breakdown). Both run the same `_get_tax_totals_summary` pipeline; do not reimplement totals by hand.
- **Combo products carry no tax on the parent line** ([sale_order_line.py:706-708](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L706)); tax lives on the component lines.
- **`_filter_taxes_by_company` is applied before mapping** ([sale_order_line.py:711-713](../addons/sale/models/sale_order_line.py#L711)). In multi-company setups a product's `taxes_id` can include other companies' taxes; only the order company's survive into the mapping step.
- **Expense reinvoicing has its own tax derivation.** Vendor-bill lines reinvoiced onto a sale order go through `_sale_prepare_sale_line_values` ([sale/account_move_line.py:227](../addons/sale/models/account_move_line.py#L227)), which re-runs filter-by-company + `map_tax` itself rather than reusing `_compute_tax_ids`.

## Taxes in Purchase (RFQ, Purchase Order, Vendor Bill)

Purchase taxes follow a simple chain: the product carries default **vendor taxes**, the purchase order line resolves them through the vendor's **fiscal position**, the order rolls them up into totals, and when you bill the order those exact taxes are copied verbatim onto the vendor bill. The accounting engine (`account.tax`) does all the actual math; `purchase` only decides *which* taxes apply and *carries them forward*. This section walks the chain in order and ends with a worked example.

### The starting point: vendor taxes on the product

Every product template has a "Purchase Taxes" field, `supplier_taxes_id`, restricted to purchase-type taxes. Its default comes from the company's configured purchase tax (falling back to the root company in a branch hierarchy), so a freshly created product is never tax-less by accident — see [product.py:86](../addons/account/models/product.py#L86).

```python
supplier_taxes_id = fields.Many2many('account.tax', 'product_supplier_taxes_rel', ...
    domain=[('type_tax_use', '=', 'purchase')],
    default=lambda self: self.env.companies.account_purchase_tax_id or self.env.companies.root_id.sudo().account_purchase_tax_id,
)
```

This is the *raw* set of taxes. It is not yet what lands on the order — the line still has to filter by company and remap through the fiscal position.

### Resolving the line's taxes: `tax_ids` on the purchase order line

The PO line field is `tax_ids` (a Many2many to `account.tax`), defined at [purchase_order_line.py:38](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L38). Note it is *not* a stored compute with a `@api.depends` chain — it is filled imperatively by `_compute_tax_id`, which is invoked from the product onchange. The flow is:

1. `onchange_product_id` fires when a product is picked ([purchase_order_line.py:503](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L503)).
2. It calls `_product_id_change`, which calls `_compute_tax_id` at [purchase_order_line.py:191](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L191).
3. `_compute_tax_id` does the real work ([purchase_order_line.py:191-197](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L191)):

```python
def _compute_tax_id(self):
    for line in self:
        line = line.with_company(line.company_id)
        fpos = line.order_id.fiscal_position_id or line.order_id.fiscal_position_id._get_fiscal_position(line.order_id.partner_id)
        taxes = line.product_id.supplier_taxes_id._filter_taxes_by_company(line.company_id)
        line.tax_ids = fpos.map_tax(taxes)
```

Two transformations happen here, and the order matters:

| Step | What it does | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `_filter_taxes_by_company` | Keep only the product's vendor taxes belonging to this company; walks up the parent-company chain until it finds a match | [account_tax.py:4967](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4967) |
| `fpos.map_tax` | Replace each surviving tax with its fiscal-position mapping (or drop it entirely for an empty fiscal position) | [partner.py:161](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L161) |

The fiscal position used is the one on the order header if set, otherwise the one auto-resolved from the vendor. `map_tax` is the substitution mechanism: a fiscal position can swap "Domestic VAT 21%" for "Intra-EU 0%" or for a reverse-charge tax. Crucially, an *empty* fiscal position (no `tax_ids`, e.g. one created by a tax unit) returns an empty recordset — it **removes every tax that names a fiscal position**, it does not leave them untouched ([partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170)).

The same two-step logic is duplicated in `_prepare_purchase_order_line` ([purchase_order_line.py:809](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L809), mapping at [purchase_order_line.py:828](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L828)), which is the programmatic path used when procurement (e.g. reordering rules, MTO) generates PO lines without going through the UI onchange:

```python
product_taxes = product_id.supplier_taxes_id.filtered_domain(tax_domain)
taxes = po.fiscal_position_id.map_tax(product_taxes)
...
'tax_ids': [(6, 0, taxes.ids)],
```

So whether a line is typed by a buyer or created by the scheduler, it ends up with the same fiscal-position-mapped, company-filtered taxes.

### Price vs. tax: handling price-included taxes

When the system pulls the cost from the vendor pricelist (`seller.price`) or the product's `standard_price`, that figure might itself be tax-inclusive. Before storing it as `price_unit`, the line calls `_fix_tax_included_price_company` ([purchase_order_line.py:564](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L564)). That helper, defined at [account_tax.py:4993](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4993) — **changed in 20**: it now also takes `document_tax_mode` —, strips out any *price-included* tax that is on the product's vendor taxes but **not** on the line's resolved `tax_ids` — so if a fiscal position mapped away an inclusive tax, its amount is correctly removed from the unit price rather than silently kept.

### Per-line amounts: `_compute_amount`

Once `tax_ids` and `price_unit` are set, each line computes its own subtotal/total via `_compute_amount` ([purchase_order_line.py:151](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L151)). It does not hand-roll arithmetic — it builds a generic "base line" dict (`_prepare_base_line_for_taxes_computation`, [purchase_order_line.py:154-160](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L154), passing `tax_ids`, quantity, partner, currency, rate) and feeds it to the shared `account.tax` engine. Results: `price_subtotal` (excl. tax), `price_total` (incl. tax), and `price_tax` (the difference).

### Order totals: `amount_untaxed`, `amount_tax`, `amount_total`

The header aggregates all non-section lines in `_amount_all` ([purchase_order.py:32](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L32)). It collects every line's base-line dict, runs the tax engine's `_add_tax_details_in_base_lines` + `_round_base_lines_tax_details`, then `_get_tax_totals_summary`, and maps the summary onto the stored monetary fields:

```python
order.amount_untaxed = tax_totals['base_amount_currency']
order.amount_tax     = tax_totals['tax_amount_currency']
order.amount_total   = tax_totals['total_amount_currency']
order.amount_total_cc = tax_totals['total_amount']   # company currency
```

`_compute_tax_totals` ([purchase_order.py:300](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L300)) runs the identical pipeline but stores the *full* structured summary in the non-stored `tax_totals` field — a `Json` field since 20, [purchase_order.py:142](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L142) — — that is what the totals widget on the form renders (the per-tax-group breakdown). The numeric `amount_*` fields are stored and tracked; `tax_totals` is recomputed for display.

Rounding is done **globally across all lines together** (`_round_base_lines_tax_details` receives the whole list), not line-by-line — this is why the order total can differ by a cent from summing rounded per-line totals.

### Carrying taxes to the vendor bill

There are two routes from PO to bill; both copy `tax_ids` byte-for-byte rather than recomputing from the product.

**Route 1 — "Create Bill" from the PO.** `action_create_invoice` ([purchase_order.py:820](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L820)) builds the move header with `_prepare_invoice` ([purchase_order.py:982](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L982)) — note it copies the PO's `fiscal_position_id` onto the bill, falling back to detection from the vendor ([purchase_order.py:994](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L994)) — then builds each invoice line with `_prepare_account_move_line` ([purchase_order_line.py:773](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L773)):

```python
'tax_ids': [(6, 0, self.tax_ids.ids)],
'purchase_line_id': self.id,
```

The taxes already resolved on the PO line are written straight onto the `account.move.line`. The fiscal position is **not re-applied** at this point — it was already applied when the PO line was created, and reapplying would double-map.

**Route 2 — Auto-complete on a manually started bill.** On a vendor bill you can pick a PO in the "Auto-complete" field; `_onchange_purchase_auto_complete` ([account_invoice.py:39](../addons/purchase/models/account_invoice.py#L39)) pulls header values via `_prepare_invoice` and adds the PO lines through `_add_purchase_order_lines` → `_prepare_account_move_line` ([account_invoice.py:325](../addons/purchase/models/account_invoice.py#L325)). Same `tax_ids` copy.

The link is bidirectional and persistent: `account.move.line.purchase_line_id` ([account_invoice.py:662](../addons/purchase/models/account_invoice.py#L662)) ties the bill line back to its PO line, and `purchase_order_id` is a related shortcut to its order ([account_invoice.py:663](../addons/purchase/models/account_invoice.py#L663)).

### Purchase-specific behavior in `purchase/models/account_tax.py`

This file is tiny and does exactly one thing: it adds a `purchase_order_line_ids` m2m and overrides `_compute_is_used` so the "is this tax used anywhere?" check also scans purchase order lines ([purchase/account_tax.py:16](../addons/purchase/models/account_tax.py#L16)). (Core's own `_compute_is_used` was simplified in 20 — the `_hook_compute_is_used` extension point is gone and the check now reads the `account_move_line_ids` / `account_reconcile_model_line_ids` m2m relations directly, [account_tax.py:334](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L334).) Without it, a tax referenced only by open PO lines (via the `account_tax_purchase_order_line_rel` relation table) would be considered unused and could be archived/deleted. It computes nothing about amounts — it only protects in-use purchase taxes.

### Worked example: deductible VAT from PO line to vendor bill

Setup: a single company, product **Office Chairs**, vendor **Acme**, no fiscal position remapping. The product's `supplier_taxes_id` = "VAT 20% (Purchases)", a standard deductible, *not* price-included tax.

1. **RFQ.** Buyer adds Office Chairs, qty 10, unit cost 100.00 (tax-excluded). `onchange_product_id` → `_compute_tax_id`:
   - `_filter_taxes_by_company` keeps "VAT 20%" (it belongs to this company).
   - `map_tax` with no/empty-mapping fiscal position returns it unchanged.
   - Line `tax_ids` = {VAT 20%}.
2. **Per-line amounts** (`_compute_amount`): base 10 × 100 = 1 000.00.
   - `price_subtotal` = 1 000.00
   - `price_tax` = 200.00
   - `price_total` = 1 200.00
3. **Order totals** (`_amount_all`):
   - `amount_untaxed` = 1 000.00
   - `amount_tax` = 200.00
   - `amount_total` = 1 200.00
4. **Confirm and bill.** `action_create_invoice` → `_prepare_account_move_line` writes the bill line with `quantity=10`, `price_unit=100.00`, `tax_ids=[VAT 20%]`, `purchase_line_id=<this PO line>`.
5. **On the vendor bill**, the `account.move` recomputes its own totals from those copied taxes: untaxed 1 000.00, VAT 200.00, total 1 200.00. Because VAT 20% (Purchases) is a deductible tax, posting the bill debits the input-VAT (tax-receivable) account for 200.00, the expense/asset account for 1 000.00, and credits the vendor payable for 1 200.00. The 200.00 sits in the deductible-VAT account, claimable on the VAT return — that "deductible" nature lives entirely in the `account.tax` configuration, not in any purchase code.

### Gotchas

- **`tax_ids` is computed imperatively — but the PO header re-maps it.** Each line's taxes are set by `_compute_tax_id` ([purchase_order_line.py:191](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L191)), driven from the product onchange and from `_prepare_purchase_order_line`. Changing the **fiscal position or company on the PO header** *does* re-map every existing line immediately: `PurchaseOrder._compute_tax_id` is an `@api.onchange('fiscal_position_id', 'company_id')` that fans out to `order_line._compute_tax_id()` ([purchase_order.py:529-530](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L529)). What it does **not** auto-remap is a purely *programmatic* write to `fiscal_position_id` (no onchange fired) — that leaves existing lines' taxes stale until the line onchange is re-triggered.
- **Empty fiscal position wipes taxes.** A fiscal position with no `tax_ids` (e.g. auto-created by a tax unit) makes `map_tax` drop every tax that names a fiscal position, so the line can end up with **zero** taxes, not the original ones. ([partner.py:170-174](../addons/account/models/partner.py#L170))
- **Company filtering walks up the hierarchy.** `_filter_taxes_by_company` returns the first non-empty match going from the line's company up through parent companies; in a branch setup a child company with no own copy of the tax inherits the parent's. ([account_tax.py:4967](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4967))
- **The bill copies taxes, it does not recompute them.** `_prepare_account_move_line` does `[(6, 0, self.tax_ids.ids)]`. Whatever (possibly hand-edited) taxes are on the PO line at billing time are exactly what the bill gets; the fiscal position is not re-applied to the line. ([purchase_order_line.py:773](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order_line.py#L773))
- **Price-included taxes need the `_fix_tax_included_price_company` step.** If a fiscal position maps away an inclusive tax, the stored `price_unit` is reduced accordingly. Setting `price_unit` directly in custom code bypasses this and can double-count an inclusive tax. ([account_tax.py:4993](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4993))
- **Global rounding.** Order and bill totals round across all lines at once, so re-deriving the total by summing each line's rounded `price_total` can be off by a cent. ([purchase_order.py:38](../addons/purchase/models/purchase_order.py#L38))
- **Don't delete a tax that's only on open POs through naive checks.** The `_compute_is_used` override in `purchase/models/account_tax.py` is the only thing that flags such taxes as in-use; custom tooling that doesn't account for the `account_tax_purchase_order_line_rel` table may wrongly treat them as orphaned. ([purchase/account_tax.py:16](../addons/purchase/models/account_tax.py#L16))

## Taxes on Journal Entries: the GL Side

Taxes in Odoo are configured on `account.tax`, but they only become *money in the books* when they turn into journal items (`account.move.line`, AML). This section explains that conversion: how a normal invoice line "carrying" taxes spawns separate tax journal items, where their amount and GL account come from, and how reporting reconstructs which base produced which tax.

### Base lines vs. tax lines

Every line of a posted move is an `account.move.line`. Two roles matter for tax:

| Role | What it is | Key fields | `display_type` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base line | A normal product/expense line that *is subject to* taxes | `tax_ids` (the taxes that apply), `tax_tag_ids` (report grids for the base) | `product` |
| Tax line | An auto-generated line holding the *tax amount itself* | `tax_line_id`, `tax_repartition_line_id`, `tax_base_amount`, `tax_tag_ids` | `tax` |

The two are mutually exclusive in meaning: a base line declares which taxes apply via `tax_ids` ([account_move_line.py:226](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L226)); a tax line is recognised by having a `tax_repartition_line_id`, from which `tax_line_id` is derived as a stored related field ([account_move_line.py:243](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L243)). `display_type` is computed: a line is `'tax'` exactly when it has a `tax_line_id` ([account_move_line.py:386](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L386), computed at [account_move_line.py:623](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L623)).

Why split them at all? Because a base amount and its tax usually hit **different GL accounts** (revenue vs. a VAT payable account) and feed **different report grids**. They must be separate journal items to post correctly and to be auditable.

`tax_repartition_line_id` points to an `account.tax.repartition.line` — the "distribution line" of the tax. A tax does not carry a single account or a single percentage; it carries an ordered list of repartition lines (one `base` line plus one or more `tax` lines, separately for the invoice and refund cases). Each `tax`-type repartition line says: post `factor_percent` of the tax to `account_id` with these `tag_ids` ([account_tax.py:5142](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5142), [account_tax.py:5152](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5152), [account_tax.py:5159](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5159)). That is why 100% VAT can be split, e.g. 50/50 between two accounts, and why a tax line always traces back to exactly one repartition line.

### How tax lines get (re)created when base lines change

Tax lines are *dynamic*: the user never types them. They are recomputed whenever base lines change, inside a context manager on `account.move`. The orchestrator `_sync_dynamic_lines` runs the tax sync only for moves that actually involve tax ([account_move.py:3816](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3816), stage 50 at [account_move.py:3801](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3801)).

The mechanism is a snapshot-diff. Before any write, the sync records the tracked tax-relevant values of every base and tax line; it `yield`s to let the write happen; then it compares (`_sync_tax_lines`, [account_move.py:3335](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3335)). The tracked base fields include the grouping key plus `price_unit`, `quantity`, `discount`, `deductible_percentage` and `extra_tax_data` — or `amount_currency` for non-invoices ([account_move.py:3351-3356](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3351)). Only draft moves are touched ([account_move.py:3414-3415](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3414)) — posted moves keep their tax lines frozen.

When something changed, Odoo collects the current base lines and existing tax lines, then calls the shared tax engine:

```python
tax_results = AccountTax._prepare_tax_lines(base_lines_values, move.company_id, tax_lines=tax_lines_values)
```

at [account_move.py:3467](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3467). `_prepare_tax_lines` returns a four-part diff — `tax_lines_to_add`, `tax_lines_to_delete`, `tax_lines_to_update`, `base_lines_to_update` ([account_tax.py:3078](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3078), contract documented at [account_tax.py:3098-3112](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3098)). The move then applies it: writes the recomputed `tax_tag_ids`/amounts back onto base lines, deletes stale tax lines, creates new ones with `display_type='tax'`, and updates surviving ones in place ([account_move.py:3518-3560](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3518)). Surviving-vs-new is decided by a **grouping key**, not by line identity (see below), so editing a quantity updates the existing VAT line instead of churning it.

The `round_from_tax_lines` flag decides the rounding direction: normally Odoo rounds from base amounts, but if a tax line was manually edited, or only the FX rate changed, it preserves the manual/foreign tax amount and works backwards ([account_move.py:3429-3461](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3429)).

### Where the amount, the account, and the tags come from

Inside `_prepare_tax_lines`, every base line's pre-computed `tax_details` is walked. For each tax, for each of its `tax`-type repartition lines, a **grouping key** is built and amounts are accumulated into a dict keyed by that frozen key ([account_tax.py:3136-3141](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3136)):

- `tax_base_amount += sign * base_amount` — the base behind this tax,
- `balance += sign * tax_amount` (company currency),
- `amount_currency += sign * tax_amount_currency`.

The grouping key is the identity of a tax line: `tax_repartition_line_id`, `partner_id`, `currency_id`, `group_tax_id`, `analytic_distribution`, `account_id`, `tax_ids`, `tax_tag_ids` ([account_tax.py:2349-2363](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2349), on top of the base-line key at [account_tax.py:2316](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2316)). Two repartition results with the same key merge into one journal item; differing keys produce separate lines. The same key shape is recomputed from existing tax lines to match them for update ([account_tax.py:2366](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2366)).

The GL account comes from the repartition line, resolved by `_get_aml_target_tax_account` ([account_tax.py:5196](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5196)): normally `tax_rep.account_id`, but for cash-basis (`on_payment`) taxes it returns the `cash_basis_transition_account_id` instead. If the repartition line has no account, the tax posts to the base line's own account ([account_tax.py:2359](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2359), `'account_id': tax_rep_data['account'].id or base_line_grouping_key['account_id']`).

`tax_tag_ids` on the tax line are the repartition line's `tag_ids` ([account_tax.py:2501-2504](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2501)); the base line separately receives the `base`-type repartition tags ([account_tax.py:2431-2432](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2431)). These tags are what tax reports group by — the amount lands in a grid because of the tag, not because of the account.

`tax_base_amount` is informational on the tax line: it records how much base produced this tax, but it is **not** a debit/credit and does not affect the balance.

### Worked example: a 2-line customer invoice (21% and 6%)

Customer invoice, both lines exclusive of tax. Line A = 1000.00 @ 21%, line B = 500.00 @ 6%. Each standard tax has one `tax` repartition line at 100% pointing to a VAT-payable account (here 251000).

Tax engine output:
- 21% on 1000.00 → 210.00
- 6% on 500.00 → 30.00
- Invoice total = 1500.00 + 240.00 = 1740.00.

Posted journal items (sale, so revenue is credited, receivable debited):

| display_type | Account | Debit | Credit | tax_ids | tax_line_id / rep line | tax_base_amount |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| product | 400000 Revenue | | 1000.00 | 21% | — | — |
| product | 400000 Revenue | | 500.00 | 6% | — | — |
| tax | 251000 VAT payable | | 210.00 | — | 21% / its tax rep | 1000.00 |
| tax | 251000 VAT payable | | 30.00 | — | 6% / its tax rep | 500.00 |
| payment_term | 121000 Receivable | 1740.00 | | — | — | — |

Two distinct tax lines exist because the grouping keys differ on `tax_repartition_line_id` (and `tax_ids`). Had both taxes shared one repartition line and account, they would still stay separate — the repartition line id differs. If you then change line A's quantity, the sync recomputes, matches the 21% key to the existing 210.00 line, and *updates* it rather than recreating it.

### account_move_line_tax_details: per-line base breakdown

`account.move.line.tax.details` is a read-only SQL helper (not a stored model — it builds a query) used by tax reports and audit to answer: *for this tax line, exactly which base lines and how much base produced it?* (`_get_query_tax_details`, [account_move_line_tax_details.py:28](../addons/account/models/account_move_line_tax_details.py#L28)).

Why not a plain `GROUP BY`? Because the link between a base line and a tax line is **many-to-many and amount-weighted**, and "tax-on-tax" breaks naive aggregation:

1. One tax line can summarise several base lines (two product lines at 21% collapse into one VAT line). A `GROUP BY` on the tax line loses the per-base split needed for grid reporting.
2. Taxes with `include_base_amount` (a tax that increases the base of the *next* tax) mean a tax line is itself part of another tax's base. The view dispatches that amount pro-rata across the originating base lines ([account_move_line_tax_details.py:204](../addons/account/models/account_move_line_tax_details.py#L204)).

The query first builds `base_tax_line_mapping`, joining each tax line back to its base lines through `account_move_line_account_tax_rel`, matching on move, currency, partner, and `COALESCE(tax_rep.account_id, base_line.account_id) = tax_line.account_id` ([account_move_line_tax_details.py:98](../addons/account/models/account_move_line_tax_details.py#L98)). It then dispatches affect-base amounts, and finally apportions each tax amount to its bases pro-rata (window functions, with `LAG` to absorb rounding) producing rows of `(base_line_id, tax_line_id, src_line_id, base_amount, tax_amount)` ([account_move_line_tax_details.py:458](../addons/account/models/account_move_line_tax_details.py#L458)). The file's own header comment walks a 3-base / 4-tax example with the affect-base case ([account_move_line_tax_details.py:86](../addons/account/models/account_move_line_tax_details.py#L86)).

If configuration changed after posting (e.g. the tax's accounts were edited) the exact join can fail; a `fallback` branch then builds an approximate mapping from `tax_ids`/`tax_line_id` alone ([account_move_line_tax_details.py:51](../addons/account/models/account_move_line_tax_details.py#L51)).

### Gotchas

- **A tax line is identified by `tax_repartition_line_id`, not `tax_line_id`.** `tax_line_id` is a stored *related* field on the repartition line ([account_move_line.py:243](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L243)); clearing the repartition line silently demotes the line out of `display_type='tax'`.
- **`tax_base_amount` is metadata, not a posting.** It never appears in debit/credit and does not balance. It exists for reporting only.
- **Tax lines only recompute on draft moves** ([account_move.py:3414-3415](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L3414)). After posting, base-line edits are blocked precisely so the GL stays consistent; a constraint also guards changing `tax_ids`/`tax_repartition_line_id` on reconciled or posted lines ([account_move_line.py:1848](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1848), [account_move_line.py:1908](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1908)).
- **The GL account is the repartition line's, not the tax's.** A single tax can post to several accounts via multiple `tax` repartition lines; an empty repartition account falls back to the base line's account ([account_tax.py:2359](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2359)).
- **Cash-basis taxes post to the transition account first**, not the final VAT account, until payment ([account_tax.py:5196-5204](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5196)).
- **Report grids follow `tax_tag_ids`, not the account.** Wrong or missing repartition `tag_ids` mean correct GL postings but an empty tax return.
- **Zero-amount tax lines are dropped** unless `__keep_zero_line` is set, so a 0% tax normally leaves no tax journal item ([account_tax.py:2362](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2362), [account_tax.py:3147](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3147)).
- **The tax-details view can return approximate numbers.** If the exact mapping fails and `fallback=True`, results are a best-effort estimate, not the posted truth ([account_move_line_tax_details.py:51](../addons/account/models/account_move_line_tax_details.py#L51)).

## Cash-Basis Taxes (Tax Exigibility)

### Why cash-basis taxes exist

"Tax exigibility" answers one question: **when does a tax become due to the authorities?** Two regimes exist.

| Regime | Code value | Tax reported when... | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Based on Invoice (accrual) | `on_invoice` | the invoice is validated | the default everywhere |
| Based on Payment (cash basis) | `on_payment` | the customer actually pays | small businesses, some service sectors, jurisdictions allowing cash VAT |

The accrual default is simple: validate an invoice with 21% VAT, and that VAT immediately lands on the real VAT payable account and on the tax report. The cash-basis regime is the harder problem Odoo has to solve: you must **collect** the tax on the invoice (the customer is billed for it) but you may not **report** it until money arrives — and if the customer pays in installments, you report the tax proportionally, payment by payment.

Odoo handles this with a holding account plus a generated journal entry at reconciliation. The tax field is defined as a Selection at [account_tax.py:149](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L149), with the help text spelling out the two meanings at [account_tax.py:153](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L153).

### The three configuration pieces

Cash basis is opt-in at the company level and then configured per tax.

**1. Company switch + dedicated journal.** A company flag `tax_exigibility` (label "Use Cash Basis") and a `tax_cash_basis_journal_id` are defined at [company.py:238-242](../addons/account/models/company.py#L238). All the generated cash-basis entries are booked in this one journal. A third field, `account_cash_basis_base_account_id` ("Base Tax Received Account"), holds the *base* amount mirror lines so the tax report can re-state the taxable base on payment — see [company.py:243-248](../addons/account/models/company.py#L243). These are surfaced in Settings as related fields at [res_config_settings.py:109](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L109).

You cannot turn the company switch off while any `on_payment` tax still exists — the onchange at [res_config_settings.py:295](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L295) forces it back on and warns the user.

**2. The transition (holding) account.** Each cash-basis tax carries `cash_basis_transition_account_id` at [account_tax.py:155](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L155). This is the heart of the mechanism: **when the invoice is posted, the tax amount is parked here instead of on the real VAT account.** It stays here until payment. The help text says it directly: the amount sits on this account "as long as the original invoice has not been reconciled; at reconciliation, this amount cancelled on this account and put on the regular tax account."

The transition account **must allow reconciliation**, because the cash-basis entry later reconciles against the invoice's transition line. **Changed in 20:** this is no longer enforced in Python — the constraint `_constrains_cash_basis_transition_account` that raised *"The cash basis transition account needs to allow reconciliation"* was removed. All that remains is the form-level `required="tax_exigibility == 'on_payment'"` on the field ([account_tax_views.xml:261](../addons/account/views/account_tax_views.xml#L261)), which does not check `account.reconcile` at all. A tax written from code or an import can therefore end up with a non-reconcilable (or missing) transition account, and the failure only shows up at reconciliation time.

**3. Routing the invoice tax line.** On invoice posting, `_get_aml_target_tax_account` decides where the tax line goes. For an `on_payment` tax it returns the transition account, not the normal tax account — see [account_tax.py:5196-5204](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5196). That is how the tax lands in the holding account in the first place.

### What gets reported when (the tag distinction)

The same routing logic controls **tax report tags**. On the original invoice, an `on_payment` tax does *not* receive its tax-grid tags — only `on_invoice` taxes do. That is the `include_caba_tags` flag in the tax-detail builder: base tags are only attached when `include_caba_tags or tax.tax_exigibility == 'on_invoice'` ([account_tax.py:2431-2432](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2431), and the same guard on tax-line tags at [account_tax.py:2502-2504](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2502)), and the account is forced to the real tax account only when `force_caba_exigibility` is set ([account_tax.py:5196](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5196)).

So on the invoice itself, the cash-basis tax is invisible to the tax report — no tags, sitting on a transition account. The tags get applied later, on the cash-basis entry generated at payment. That is why `include_caba_tags=True` is the signal meaning "we are building the cash-basis entry, so put the real report tags on now."

### How reconciliation triggers the cash-basis entry

The whole flow fires from reconciliation, not from posting. When move lines are reconciled, the plan builder checks `is_cash_basis_needed` (company has cash basis on, and the line is receivable/payable) and, if so, calls `_create_tax_cash_basis_moves()` on the partials — see [account_move_line.py:3211-3220](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L3211). Note it is skipped during `move_reverse_cancel`.

Two collection steps run:

1. **Per move, gather what to process.** `_collect_tax_cash_basis_values` at [account_move.py:4222](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4222) walks the invoice lines. Receivable/payable lines feed `total_balance` / `total_residual` (the denominator for the paid percentage); each tax line whose `tax_line_id.tax_exigibility == 'on_payment'` is tagged `('tax', line)` at [account_move.py:4267-4269](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4267); each base line carrying an `on_payment` tax is tagged `('base', line)` at [account_move.py:4271-4273](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4271). It also computes `is_fully_paid` at [account_move.py:4286](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4286). Multi-currency moves with mixed currencies are not supported and return `None`.

2. **Per partial, compute the paid percentage.** `_collect_tax_cash_basis_values` on the partial-reconcile model at [account_partial_reconcile.py:243](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L243) divides the partial's amount by the move's total balance: `percentage = partial_amount / move_values['total_balance']` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:313](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L313) (or by `total_amount_currency` when the partial is in foreign currency, [account_partial_reconcile.py:320](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L320)). This percentage is what makes the tax move proportionally.

Then `_create_tax_cash_basis_moves` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:527](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L527) builds the entry. For each `to_process_line`, the cash-basis amount is `line.amount_currency * percentage` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:571](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L571). It creates two mirrored line pairs:

- **Base mirror** (`_prepare_cash_basis_base_line_vals` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:358](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L358)): debit and credit on the `account_cash_basis_base_account_id` (falling back to the base line's own account), net zero, carrying the **base tax tags** [account_partial_reconcile.py:368-373](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L368). This re-states the taxable base on the report without affecting the P&L.
- **Tax move** — two lines. The **main tax line** (`_prepare_cash_basis_tax_line_vals` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:411](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L411)) posts to the **real tax account** — `tax_repartition_line_id.account_id` — and carries `tax_repartition_line_id` plus the real report tags ([account_partial_reconcile.py:420-423](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L420)), so *this* is the entry the tax report sees. Its **counterpart** (`_prepare_cash_basis_counterpart_tax_line_vals` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:442](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L442)) flips debit/credit and posts to the **transition account** — `tax_line.account_id`, where the invoice originally parked the tax ([account_partial_reconcile.py:455](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L455)) — cancelling the parked amount.

After creation, the transition-account legs are reconciled back against the invoice's original transition line at [account_partial_reconcile.py:687-706](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L687). When the invoice is fully paid, the last partial absorbs any rounding remainder via the `is_fully_paid` adjustment at [account_partial_reconcile.py:576](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L576), guaranteeing the transition account nets to zero. The generated move is linked back through `tax_cash_basis_rec_id` and `tax_cash_basis_origin_move_id` set at [account_partial_reconcile.py:542-545](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L542).

### Worked example: 21% on_payment tax, invoice partially paid 50%

Customer invoice: 1000 base + 210 VAT (21%, `on_payment`) = 1210 total. The VAT tax is cash basis; its transition account is `134000 VAT to be received`.

**On invoice validation** (accrual side untouched, tax parked):

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 411000 Receivable | 1210 | |
| 700000 Revenue | | 1000 |
| 134000 VAT Transition (cash basis) | | 210 |

The 210 is **not** on `251000 VAT Payable` and carries **no** report tags. The tax report shows nothing yet.

**Customer pays 605 (50%).** The bank entry reconciles 605 against the receivable. `percentage = 605 / 1210 = 0.50`. The cash-basis journal generates (booked in `tax_cash_basis_journal_id`):

| Account | Debit | Credit | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 99999 Base account | 500 | | base mirror (one leg) |
| 99999 Base account | | 500 | base mirror counterpart — net 0, carries base tags |
| 134000 VAT Transition | 105 | | release 50% of parked tax |
| 251000 VAT Payable | | 105 | now exigible — carries tax report tags |

The 105 debit on the transition account is reconciled against the original 210 credit, leaving 105 still parked. The tax report now shows 105 of output VAT on a base of 500 — exactly the paid half.

**Remaining 605 paid later:** an identical entry fires for the second 50%, releasing the final 105 and clearing the transition account to zero. Total VAT reported across both payments = 210, matching the invoice.

### Gotchas

- **No company switch = no entries.** `is_cash_basis_needed` requires the company `tax_exigibility` flag; an `on_payment` tax in a company without the switch will park tax on the transition account forever with no release entry. The check is at [account_move_line.py:3211-3213](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L3211).
- **Missing journal is a hard error.** If no `tax_cash_basis_journal_id` is configured, reconciliation raises a `UserError` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:280](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L280). Configure the journal before using cash-basis taxes.
- **Changed in 20: nothing validates the transition account any more.** In 19 saving an `on_payment` tax whose transition account was not reconcilable raised a `ValidationError` (except under the `chart_template_load` context). That constraint was removed; only the form's `required` attribute remains ([account_tax_views.xml:261](../addons/account/views/account_tax_views.xml#L261)). Check the account's **Allow Reconciliation** flag yourself when configuring cash-basis taxes.
- **The invoice never carries the report tags for cash-basis taxes.** Only `on_invoice` taxes are tagged on the source document ([account_tax.py:2431-2432](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2431)). If you grep the invoice's tax lines expecting grid tags, they are absent by design — they live on the generated cash-basis entry.
- **Mixed foreign currencies bail out.** `_collect_tax_cash_basis_values` returns `None` when more than one currency is involved ([account_move.py:4278-4283](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4278)); the cash-basis entry is simply not created in that case.
- **Reversals skip generation.** Cash-basis entries are not created under the `move_reverse_cancel` or `no_cash_basis` contexts ([account_move_line.py:3215](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L3215)); when a reconciliation is undone, the matching cash-basis entries are reversed instead (the link survives via `tax_cash_basis_origin_move_id`, [account_move.py:278](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L278)).
- **Rounding lands on the last payment.** Intermediate partials use the raw `amount * percentage`; only the final partial of a fully-paid move forces the residual to clear the transition account ([account_partial_reconcile.py:576](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L576)). Do not expect each installment's tax to be a perfectly clean fraction.
- **Draft vs posted source.** The cash-basis move is posted immediately only when both reconciled moves are posted (`both_move_posted` at [account_partial_reconcile.py:345](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L345), applied at [account_partial_reconcile.py:672-681](../addons/account/models/account_partial_reconcile.py#L672)); otherwise it is created in draft.

## Withholding Taxes

### What withholding tax is

A withholding tax is an amount you **retain from a payment** instead of paying it to the supplier (or instead of collecting it from a customer). You then owe that retained amount to the tax authority. It is common for service invoices and payments to non-residents, where the buyer is legally the one who must remit a slice of the price as tax.

The key difference from a normal tax is **timing**. A normal VAT line is recognized when the invoice is posted. A withholding tax must **not** touch your accounts at invoice time. It only becomes a journal entry when the **payment** is registered, because withholding is an event that happens at payment, not at billing. The module `l10n_account_withholding_tax` ("Withholding Tax on Payment", depends only on `account`) implements exactly this generic behavior, and localizations build on top of it.

> **Changed in 20 — renamed flag and a visible invoice figure.** The tax flag `is_withholding_tax_on_payment` was **renamed to `is_withholding_tax`** ([account_tax.py:13](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L13)) and its label went from "Withhold On Payment" to just "Withhold". More importantly, withholding is no longer invisible until you open the payment wizard: `account.move` now carries `withholding_total_amount_currency`, `withholding_deducted_amount_currency`, `withholding_residual_amount_currency` and the user-facing **Net Due Amount** `withholding_net_residual_amount_currency` ([account_move.py:8-34](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_move.py#L8)), so an invoice shows what will be withheld and what the supplier will actually receive. The residual field is one of the new v20 `compute_sql` fields — non-stored but searchable and groupable in SQL ([account_move.py:104-105](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_move.py#L104)). A new `withhold` selector ("Withhold and Pay" / "Withhold Only") on the payment and the register wizard lets you post the withholding entry on its own ([account_payment.py:13](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment.py#L13), [account_payment_register.py:14](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L14)).

### Marking a tax as withholding

A withholding tax is an ordinary `account.tax` record with one extra flag, `is_withholding_tax` ([account_tax.py:13](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L13)). Two rules make these taxes behave correctly:

| Rule | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tax `amount` must be negative | **Changed in 20:** ticking the flag now *flips the sign for you* — `_onchange_is_withholding_tax` sets `amount = -abs(amount)` ([account_tax.py:29-37](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L29)), and `_onchange_amount` only un-ticks the flag when the amount is **strictly** positive ([account_tax.py:39-43](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L39)). The field itself is shown for any sale/purchase tax ([account_tax_views.xml:9](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/views/account_tax_views.xml#L9)) rather than being hidden on a non-negative amount | A withheld amount *reduces* what you pay, so the tax is modeled as a negative rate (e.g. -10%) |
| No `group` or `division` amount types | `_check_amount_type` constraint ([account_tax.py:45](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L45)) | The payment-time computation only supports simple percentage/fixed math |
| Forced to `on_invoice` exigibility, `tax_excluded` | `_onchange_is_withholding_tax` ([account_tax.py:29](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L29)); the form also hides `tax_exigibility`, `price_include_override` and the cash-basis account for withholding taxes ([account_tax_views.xml:13-22](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/views/account_tax_views.xml#L13)) | Cash-basis logic would conflict with the payment-time posting this module already does |

A second field, `withholding_sequence_id` ([account_tax.py:17](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L17)), points to an `ir.sequence` used to auto-number the withholding certificate on each payment line (shown only for purchase taxes — [account_tax_views.xml:12](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/views/account_tax_views.xml#L12)).

The crucial trick that keeps withholding out of the *amount due* is in `_add_tax_details_in_base_line` ([account_tax.py:73-82](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L73)): unless the caller explicitly passes `calculate_withholding_taxes=True`, a `filter_tax_function` is injected that **drops every withholding tax** from the computation. So the invoice's own tax summary and `amount_total` never include withholding. **Changed in 20:** the move *does* now opt in on purpose, in a separate pass, to compute `withholding_total_amount_currency` for display ([account_move.py:37-68](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_move.py#L37)) — so the figure is visible on the invoice even though it never reaches the totals or the ledger until a payment is registered.

### From invoice line to payment: the withholding line

The shared logic lives in an abstract model, `account.withholding.line` ([account_withholding_line.py:10](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L10)). It has two concrete subclasses that differ only in which "comodel" they read amounts from:

| Model | Stored? | Comodel | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| `account.payment.withholding.line` | yes (persisted on the posted payment) | `account.payment` | [account_payment_withholding_line.py:9](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment_withholding_line.py#L9) |
| `account.payment.register.withholding.line` | transient (wizard) | `account.payment.register` | [account_payment_register.py:24](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L24) |

When you open the **Register Payment** wizard on a bill, `_compute_withholding_line_ids` ([account_payment_register.py:155](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L155)) reads the invoice's base lines (`move._get_rounded_base_and_tax_lines()`) and calls `_prepare_withholding_lines_commands` ([account_withholding_line.py:453](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L453)). **New in 20:** the wizard also picks a default `withhold` mode from the invoice's withholding residual — "Withhold and Pay" when there is still a net to pay, "Withhold Only" when the whole remaining balance is withholding ([account_payment_register.py:72-80](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L72)). That method recomputes the taxes **with** `calculate_withholding_taxes=True`, so this time the withholding taxes are included, and produces one withholding line per (tax, account, name) group. This is how a withholding tax set on an invoice line resurfaces as a proposed deduction at payment time.

Each line carries a `base_amount` and a withholding `amount`. The amount is derived from the original tax amount scaled by how much of the base is actually being paid ([account_withholding_line.py:225-236](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L225)). A `comodel_percentage_paid_factor` ([account_withholding_line.py:93](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L93), computed at [account_withholding_line.py:258](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L258)) lets the wizard scale the base down for partial payments, installments, or early-payment discounts. The wizard also surfaces a **Net Amount** = payment amount − sum of withholding amounts ([account_payment_register.py:85-95](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L85)); that net is what the supplier actually receives.

### How withholding lines post

The accounting impact is built in `_prepare_withholding_amls_create_values` ([account_withholding_line.py:348](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L348)). For each line it produces **four** journal items: the withholding **tax** line (the liability owed to the authority), and a **base / base-counterpart** pair that nets to zero but exists purely to carry the tax tags needed for tax reporting. The base account defaults to the company's `withholding_tax_base_account_id` if set ([account_withholding_line.py:481](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L481)).

**Changed in 20:** the splice points are now `_prepare_move_withholding_lines` ([account_payment.py:124](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment.py#L124)) and `_prepare_move_liquidity_lines` ([account_payment.py:147](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment.py#L147)), hooked into `_generate_journal_entry` ([account_payment.py:131](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment.py#L131)); the v19 `_synchronize_to_moves` / `_generate_move_vals` overrides are gone. The liquidity (bank/outstanding) line is **shrunk by each withheld amount** ([account_payment.py:153-156](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment.py#L153)), so the cash leaving the bank equals the net while the full invoice gets reconciled. A "Withhold Only" payment produces no liquidity line at all ([account_payment.py:127-129](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment.py#L127), [account_payment.py:158-159](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_payment.py#L158)).

If the payment line is added through the register wizard, `_create_payment_vals_from_wizard` ([account_payment_register.py:281](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L281)) copies the wizard lines onto the real payment and sets the outstanding account. It also blocks a negative net amount ([account_payment_register.py:293-294](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L293)).

### Worked example

Vendor bill for a service: **1000** base, with a **-10% withholding tax** on the line. The bill posts as a normal 1000 payable — the withholding tax is filtered out of the totals and has zero ledger effect yet ([account_tax.py:80-81](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L80)). In 20 the bill nevertheless *displays* a withholding total of 100 and a **Net Due Amount** of 900 ([account_move.py:29-34](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_move.py#L29)).

You register full payment. The wizard proposes one withholding line: base 1000, amount **100**. Net Amount shows **900**. On confirmation the payment move (per the docstring at [account_withholding_line.py:351-356](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L351)) is:

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Outstanding / Bank | | 900 |
| Accounts Payable | 1000 | |
| Withholding tax payable | | 100 |
| WHT base | | 1000 |
| WHT base counterpart | 1000 | |

Cash out is 900, the 1000 payable is fully reconciled, and 100 sits as a liability you owe the tax authority. The base/counterpart pair nets to zero but carries the tax grid tags.

(The core docstring at [account_withholding_line.py:351-356](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L351) shows the same example from the **customer-invoice** side — Outstanding +900 against Receivable -1000 — so its signs are the mirror of the vendor-bill table above.)

### The posted payment still shows the gross amount

The most common "why did Odoo not deduct it?" confusion. `account.payment.amount` is the **gross amount settled against the invoice**, not the cash amount — it must stay 1000, otherwise the bill would keep a 100 residual open forever. The netting happens only inside the move, by shrinking the liquidity line.

| Where you look | Shows | Field |
|---|---|---|
| Invoice / bill form (**new in 20**) | 900 (**Net Due Amount**) | `withholding_net_residual_amount_currency` ([account_move.py:29](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_move.py#L29)) |
| Register Payment wizard | 900 (**Net Amount**) | `withholding_net_amount` ([account_payment_register.py:30](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L30)) |
| Posted payment form, Amount | 1000 (gross) | `account.payment.amount` |
| Posted payment, Withholding tab | 100 withheld | `withholding_line_ids` |
| Journal Items / bank reconciliation | 900 | the liquidity line |

There is **no** net field on the posted payment — `withholding_net_amount` exists on the register wizard only, and the invoice-side `withholding_net_residual_amount_currency` is a *residual*, not a per-payment figure. So after posting, the amount actually leaving the bank is visible only on the liquidity journal item, and that is the figure a bank statement line must match.

Corollary for integrations: any code or bank-file export that reads `payment.amount` will send the **gross**. The net must be taken from the liquidity line (`payment._seek_for_lines()[0]`), not from `amount`.

### Gotchas

- **The tax rate is negative.** A 10% withholding is entered as `amount = -10`. **Changed in 20:** ticking "Withhold" now negates the amount for you, and un-ticking restores the positive sign ([account_tax.py:29-37](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L29)); a *strictly* positive amount still un-checks the flag ([account_tax.py:39-43](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L39)).
- **Nothing happens at invoice time.** Withholding taxes are stripped from invoice totals by a `filter_tax_function` unless `calculate_withholding_taxes=True` is explicitly passed ([account_tax.py:80-81](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L80)). Do not expect them in the invoice's tax summary — but since 20 *do* expect them in the invoice's separate withholding fields and in the Net Due Amount ([account_move.py:8-34](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_move.py#L8)).
- **The posted payment's Amount is the gross, not the net.** It must equal the settled invoice amount so the invoice reconciles to zero; the withholding is netted only out of the liquidity line. Read the cash figure from the journal items, never from `payment.amount`. See [The posted payment still shows the gross amount](#the-posted-payment-still-shows-the-gross-amount).
- **The WHT base pair lands on the expense account when `withholding_tax_base_account_id` is unset.** The +X/-X pair nets to zero but inflates the gross turnover of every expense account you withhold on. Set the company's withholding tax base account.
- **`group` and `division` amount types are forbidden** by constraint ([account_tax.py:45-50](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_tax.py#L45)).
- **The line account cannot be a liquidity account** (journal default, payment, outstanding, or transfer account), or the resulting entry breaks — enforced by `_constrains_account_id` ([account_withholding_line.py:300-305](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L300)).
- **Base amount must be strictly positive** ([account_withholding_line.py:293-298](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L293)).
- **A missing certificate number with no sequence raises an error** before any sequence is consumed; sequences are only burned once all names are validated ([account_withholding_line.py:369-370](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L369)).
- **Net amount cannot go negative** — withholding cannot exceed the payment ([account_payment_register.py:293-294](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L293)). With the new "Withhold Only" mode the net is forced to zero instead ([account_payment_register.py:92-95](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L92)).
- **Each withholding line posts four journal items**, not one. The base/base-counterpart pair nets to zero and exists only to carry tax tags for reporting ([account_withholding_line.py:407-417](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L407)).
- **Refunds invert direction.** Whether a line is treated as a refund depends on the cross of `type_tax_use` and payment type ([account_withholding_line.py:547](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/models/account_withholding_line.py#L547)); the wizard also flips the payment type when matching taxes for refunds ([account_payment_register.py:141](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L141)).
- **Grouped / multi-entry payments hide the feature.** If the wizard would create multiple payments (`not can_edit_wizard`, or grouped payments not consolidated), `display_withholding` is forced false ([account_payment_register.py:151-152](../addons/l10n_account_withholding_tax/wizards/account_payment_register.py#L151)).

## Configuration, Rounding & Multi-Company

Tax behaviour in Odoo is driven by a small set of company-level settings, not by per-tax flags alone. These settings decide which taxes seed new products, how cents are rounded across an invoice, whether prices include tax by default, when tax becomes due (invoice vs. payment), and which taxes are even visible in a multi-company database. All of them live on `res.company` and are surfaced (as `related` fields) in the Accounting settings via `res.config.settings`. This section explains each one, how they connect, and the rounding traps that produce one-cent discrepancies.

### Where the settings live

The user edits taxes under **Accounting → Configuration → Settings**, but every field there is just a proxy. The real storage is `res.company`; `res.config.settings` declares each setting as `related='company_id.<field>'` so saving the settings form writes straight to the active company. This matters for multi-company: each company has its own defaults, rounding method and cash-basis flag.

| Setting (UI) | `res.company` field | Default |
|---|---|---|
| Default Sales Tax | `account_sale_tax_id` | from chart template |
| Default Purchase Tax | `account_purchase_tax_id` | from chart template |
| Default Sales Price Include | `account_price_include` | `tax_excluded` |
| Rounding Method | `tax_calculation_rounding_method` | `round_globally` |
| Cash Basis | `tax_exigibility` | off |

### Default sales / purchase tax — why they exist

A product with no tax produces an invoice line with no tax, which is almost never what you want. So the company carries a fallback: `account_sale_tax_id` and `account_purchase_tax_id`. These are **not** applied automatically to every invoice line — they are used to *seed products*. When a chart template loads, it sets the company defaults from the first matching tax and then pushes those defaults onto products that previously had a tax in another company. See [chart_template.py:763-770](../addons/account/models/chart_template.py#L763): the company default is picked with `('type_tax_use', 'in', ('sale', 'all'))`, then `_force_default_sale_tax` / `_force_default_purchase_tax` propagate it onto products at [chart_template.py:775-790](../addons/account/models/chart_template.py#L775). The `check_company=True` on the config fields ([res_config_settings.py:44](../addons/account/models/res_config_settings.py#L44)) ensures you can only pick a tax belonging to that company.

### Price-included default

`account_price_include` (`tax_included` / `tax_excluded`) sets whether the price typed on products and invoices already contains tax for that company. It is the company-wide default a new tax inherits, and — **new in 20** — the default every new invoice, sale order and purchase order copies into its own `document_tax_mode`. **Changed in 20:** the v19 guard that refused to change it once the company had booked entries (`_check_set_account_price_include`) was **removed**; because each document now stores its own mode, changing the setting cannot reinterpret existing prices. Field at [company.py:294](../addons/account/models/company.py#L294).

### Rounding: round per line vs. round globally

`tax_calculation_rounding_method` has two values, and the *labels are deliberately swapped from the internal keys* — read carefully:

| Internal key | UI label | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `round_per_line` | "Round per Line" | round each line's tax to currency precision, then sum |
| `round_globally` | "Round per Tax" | keep full-precision per-line amounts, sum, round the total once, then redistribute the rounding delta back to lines |

Defined at [company.py:132-135](../addons/account/models/company.py#L132). The default is `round_globally`.

**How each one is computed.** The entry point is `_add_tax_details_in_base_line` ([account_tax.py:1723](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1723)), which takes the method from the company and forwards it to `_get_tax_details` ([account_tax.py:1745-1760](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1745)).

- Under **round per line**, the base and each tax amount are rounded immediately. The raw base is rounded at [account_tax.py:1211-1213](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1211); each tax amount at [account_tax.py:1163-1165](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1163); the per-line totals at [account_tax.py:1780-1790](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1780).
- Under **round globally**, nothing is rounded per line. Lines keep full precision, then `_round_tax_details_tax_amounts` ([account_tax.py:1916](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1916)) rounds the *total per tax* and dispatches the difference back across the lines via `_distribute_delta_amount_smoothly` ([account_tax.py:1848](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1848)). The final copy of rounded values onto the base lines happens in `_round_base_lines_tax_details` ([account_tax.py:2204](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2204)).

The reason round globally is the default in most localizations is that it makes the *invoice total* match what a customer computes from `total × rate`, even when individual line cents do not add up exactly. Round per line guarantees each printed line is internally consistent but the document total can drift by a cent or two.

### Worked example — round per line vs round globally differ by a cent

Built so each line's tax is exactly `0.125`, a perfect HALF-UP tie. Setup: EUR (2-digit precision), one 10% tax, three lines, each with a tax base of `1.25`.

| Step | Round per Line | Round Globally |
|---|---|---|
| Raw tax per line (1.25 × 10%) | 0.125 | 0.125 |
| Round each line (HALF-UP) | 0.125 → **0.13** | not rounded yet |
| Sum of line taxes | 0.13 × 3 = **0.39** | 0.125 × 3 = 0.375 |
| Round the sum (HALF-UP) | already rounded → 0.39 | 0.375 → **0.38** |
| **Total tax** | **0.39** | **0.38** |

| | Untaxed | Tax | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Round per Line | 3.75 | 0.39 | **4.14** |
| Round Globally | 3.75 | 0.38 | **4.13** |

HALF-UP rounds half **away from zero**, so each `0.125` becomes `0.13` and the three lines sum to `0.39`. Under round-globally the raw taxes sum to `0.375`, and a single HALF-UP rounding gives `0.38` — a **1-cent difference** on the same three lines, purely from the method. In global mode Odoo then spreads the `-0.01` delta back onto one line via `_round_tax_details_tax_amounts` ([account_tax.py:1916](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1916)), so two lines keep `0.13` and one carries `0.12`, still summing to `0.38`.

> **Rounding direction is HALF-UP, not banker's / half-even.** That is `float_round`'s default — see [float_utils.py:76](../odoo/tools/float_utils.py#L76) and the `match rounding_method` branch at [float_utils.py:134](../odoo/tools/float_utils.py#L134). Checking expected totals with Python's native `round()` (HALF-EVEN) is a common source of false "Odoo rounding bug" reports: HALF-EVEN would give `0.12` per line here, not `0.13`.

### Cash basis (tax exigibility)

By default tax is due when the invoice is posted. Cash basis defers VAT recognition until payment. The transition account should allow reconciliation, but **as of 20 nothing checks it** — the Python constraint was removed and only the form's `required` attribute survives. ### Multi-company: country, company domain, tax filtering

Three mechanisms keep taxes isolated and country-correct.

**`country_id` on the tax.** Every tax has a required `country_id` ([account_tax.py:182](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L182)), defaulting from the company's fiscal country (`account_fiscal_country_id`) via `_compute_country_id` at [account_tax.py:275](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L275). This is what lets a single company hold taxes for several countries (foreign VAT registrations) and what the uniqueness constraint keys on at [account_tax.py:219](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L219).

**`_check_company_domain`.** The generic ORM helper ([models.py:3339](../odoo/orm/models.py#L3339)) returns `company_id in [<companies>, False]`; `account.tax` and `account.tax.group` both override it to the parent-of variant ([account_tax.py:63](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L63), [account_tax.py:30](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L30)). Every tax search in chart loading and OSS mapping prefixes its domain with `*self.env['account.tax']._check_company_domain(company)` (e.g. [chart_template.py:320-322](../addons/account/models/chart_template.py#L320)) so it only ever returns taxes owned by that company (or company-shared `False` taxes).

**`_filter_taxes_by_company`.** When a product's tax set spans companies, this helper ([account_tax.py:4967](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L4967)) walks *up* the company hierarchy: it first keeps taxes for the exact company, and if none match, climbs to the parent company. This is how branch companies fall back to their parent's taxes.

**Chart templates load country-specific taxes.** Loading a chart for a company sets that company's fiscal country and stamps `country_id` onto created taxes ([chart_template.py:501-514](../addons/account/models/chart_template.py#L501)), then sets the company sale/purchase defaults and seeds products — all scoped through `_check_company_domain`. So installing the French chart on company A and the German chart on company B yields two disjoint, country-tagged tax sets in one database.

### Gotchas

- **The rounding labels are inverted vs. the stored keys.** The UI label "Round per Tax" stores `round_globally`, and "Round per Line" stores `round_per_line` ([company.py:132-135](../addons/account/models/company.py#L132)). Code that branches on the value must use the keys, not the labels.
- **Default sales/purchase tax do not auto-apply to invoices.** They only seed products and are the company fallback; an existing product or invoice line keeps whatever tax it already has.
- **Changed in 20: `account_price_include` is no longer frozen after first posting.** The v19 `ValidationError` is gone; the setting only seeds `document_tax_mode` on new documents, and existing ones keep the mode they were created with. The corollary is that you can no longer read the company setting to know how an old invoice was priced — read that invoice's `document_tax_mode` ([account_move.py:641](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L641)).
- **Round globally can leave per-line tax cents that look "wrong".** A line may show 1.51 where 1.52 was expected; that is the smoothed delta, and the *document* total is the correct one. Do not re-round `total_excluded` yourself — use the `raw_*` fields for high-precision needs (EDI), per the worked note at [account_tax.py:2222-2240](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2222).
- **Changed in 20: OSS mapping no longer needs tax-group payable/receivable accounts.** In 19 a domestic tax group without `tax_payable_account_id` made `_map_eu_taxes` raise a `RedirectWarning`. Those fields no longer exist on `account.tax.group`, the check is gone, and the OSS accounts come from `_get_oss_account` ([res_company.py:160](../addons/l10n_eu_oss/models/res_company.py#L160)).
- **OSS taxes attach to the first branch company that has a VAT number**, defaulting to the root company ([res_company.py:35](../addons/l10n_eu_oss/models/res_company.py#L35)) — not necessarily the company whose settings button you clicked.
- **`country_id` is required and auto-computed from the fiscal country.** If a company has no `account_fiscal_country_id` and no `country_id`, tax creation fails the `required=True` constraint ([account_tax.py:182-188](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L182)).
- **Cash basis is auto-enabled by chart loading.** Installing a localization that ships `on_payment` taxes flips `tax_exigibility` on for the company ([chart_template.py:785](../addons/account/models/chart_template.py#L785)); you may not have toggled it yourself.

## Taxes in Point of Sale (POS)

POS does not invent a new tax system. Every POS line carries the *same* `account.tax` records your invoices use, runs them through the *same* computation engine, and posts them to the *same* tax grids when the session closes. What is POS-specific is only the *plumbing around* the engine: a lightweight on-screen totals summary, a config-driven fiscal-position selector for the cashier, and one consolidated entry per session instead of one move per order.

> **Changed in 20 — two structural moves.** (1) `pos.order.line` was split out of `pos_order.py` into its own file, [pos_order_line.py](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py); every line-level anchor below points there now. (2) The session closing entry is no longer a hand-assembled `move_type='entry'` built by `_accumulate_amounts` + a direct `_prepare_tax_lines` call. `_prepare_session_move_vals` now creates an **`out_invoice` / `out_refund`** move on the config's `closing_journal_id` ([pos_session.py:977-993](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L977), [pos_session.py:995](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L995)), so the ordinary `account.move` tax synchronisation generates the tax lines — one less place where POS could drift from invoicing.

### What a POS line stores

A `pos.order.line` holds taxes in `tax_ids`, a `Many2many` to `account.tax` defined at [pos_order_line.py:46](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L46). These are populated from the product's `taxes_id` when the product is added, filtered by company ([pos_order_line.py:190](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L190)). So a line stores the unmapped product taxes, exactly like a `sale.order.line` or `account.move.line` does.

The line also exposes a computed `tax_ids_after_fiscal_position` at [pos_order_line.py:54](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L54). This is the *effective* tax after the order's fiscal position has remapped it. **New in 20:** the line also carries an `extra_tax_data` Json blob ([pos_order_line.py:71](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L71)), the same pinning mechanism sale order lines use.

### Fiscal position remapping (mirrors invoices)

The remap logic is one line, [pos_order_line.py:213-215](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L213):

```python
line.tax_ids_after_fiscal_position = line.order_id.fiscal_position_id.map_tax(line.tax_ids)
```

This is the identical `fiscal_position.map_tax()` call that invoices use. The order-level `fiscal_position_id` (defined at [pos_order.py:295](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L295)) drives it, and that field is seeded from the POS config's default when the order is created — [pos_order.py:499](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L499):

```python
values.setdefault('fiscal_position_id', session.config_id.default_fiscal_position_id.id)
```

Everywhere a line's price or tax is computed, it is `tax_ids_after_fiscal_position` (not raw `tax_ids`) that is fed to the engine: `compute_all` in [pos_order_line.py:208](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L208) and [pos_order_line.py:255](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L255), and the base-line builder that drives the accounting entry passes `tax_ids=line.tax_ids_after_fiscal_position` at [pos_order_line.py:317](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L317). The same builder also remaps the income account through `fiscal_position.map_account(account)` — [pos_order_line.py:307-308](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L307) — so a fiscal position changes both the tax and the GL account, just as on an invoice.

### POS config: which fiscal positions a cashier can pick

Two config fields control fiscal positions:

| Field | Purpose | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| `default_fiscal_position_id` | Applied to every new order by default | [pos_config.py:162](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_config.py#L162) |
| `fiscal_position_ids` | The list the cashier may switch between at the register | [pos_config.py:161](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_config.py#L161) |

The `fiscal_position_ids` set is the cashier-selectable list — the help text cites restaurants offering on-site vs take-away tax rates. ### The POS-specific totals path

Order totals are not computed by re-implementing tax logic. `_get_order_tax_totals` builds base-line dicts from the lines, enriches them with the engine, then calls the shared summarizer `_get_tax_totals_summary` ([pos_order.py:420](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L420)); `_compute_prices` just reads the result ([pos_order.py:444-456](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L444)):

```python
base_lines = order.lines._prepare_base_lines_for_taxes_computation()   # pos_order_line.py:286
AccountTax._add_tax_details_in_base_lines(base_lines, order.company_id)
AccountTax._round_base_lines_tax_details(base_lines, order.company_id)
tax_totals = AccountTax._get_tax_totals_summary(base_lines=..., currency=..., company=..., cash_rounding=...)
order.amount_tax = refund_factor * tax_totals['tax_amount_currency']
order.amount_total = refund_factor * amount_total
```

`_get_tax_totals_summary` is the same `account.tax` helper the invoice form uses for its totals block. POS only adds the `cash_rounding` argument (taken from the config's rounding method when a cash/bank payment is present) and a refund sign. So the "summary" is POS-specific glue; the numbers inside it come from the central engine.

### How POS taxes land in accounting (session close)

POS posts **one** move per closed session, not one per order — and **changed in 20**, that move is a customer invoice rather than a miscellaneous entry.

`_create_session_account_move` ([pos_session.py:995](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L995)) asks the orders for aggregated product lines and payment lines, then creates a single `out_invoice` / `out_refund` ([pos_session.py:1040-1064](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L1040)):

```python
lines = orders._prepare_account_move_line_data()            # aggregated product lines
payments = orders._prepare_account_move_line_data_for_payments()
move_vals = self._prepare_session_move_vals(orders)         # move_type out_invoice/out_refund
move_vals.update({'invoice_line_ids': ..., 'line_ids': ..., 'invoice_cash_rounding_id': ...})
move = self.env['account.move'].sudo().create(move_vals)
```

The **tax lines are no longer built by POS**. Because the move is an invoice, `account.move`'s own `_sync_tax_lines` runs the repartition and produces the tax journal items — the same code path every customer invoice uses. What POS still does itself is the *base* side: `_prepare_account_move_line_data` runs the engine per order (`_add_tax_details_in_base_lines` → `_round_base_lines_tax_details` → `_add_accounting_data_in_base_lines_tax_details(include_caba_tags=True)`) and then collapses the lines with `_reduce_base_lines_with_grouping_function` ([pos_order.py:1349-1383](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L1349), [pos_order.py:1242-1262](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L1242)). The grouping key is `(account_id, product_id, tax_ids)` — with `product_id` included only when the config's `use_closing_entry_by_product` is on ([pos_order.py:1206-1213](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L1206), [pos_config.py:206](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_config.py#L206)). Rounding is done **per order**, before aggregation, so a session cannot smear one order's rounding onto another.

Invoiced POS orders are excluded — `_get_invoiced_and_non_invoiced_orders` ([pos_session.py:1137-1143](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L1137)) splits them out, and the close refuses to run while any of their invoices is unposted ([pos_session.py:1145-1153](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L1145)).

### Shared vs POS-specific — precisely

| Concern | Shared with invoices | POS-specific |
|---|---|---|
| Tax records (`account.tax`) | Yes — same records | — |
| Computation engine (`compute_all`, `_sync_tax_lines`, base-line pipeline) | Yes | — |
| Repartition lines + tax accounts | Yes — since 20 via the standard invoice sync, not a POS copy | — |
| Tax grids / `tax_tag_ids` | Yes | — |
| Fiscal position remap (`map_tax`, `map_account`) | Yes | Source is `pos.config`, not the partner default |
| On-screen / order totals | — | `_get_tax_totals_summary` wrapper + cash rounding |
| Posting granularity | — | One consolidated session invoice, base lines grouped by `(account, product?, taxes)` |

So the Overview's claim holds: a given tax behaves **identically** in POS and on invoices because both call the same engine, repartition, and grids — and in 20 the session entry literally *is* an invoice, so the overlap is larger than it was. The only POS divergences are the totals summary it shows the cashier and the base-line aggregation when posting.

### Worked example: tax-included 18% in retail

Retail commonly uses `price_include` taxes so shelf prices are round. Say a product sells for **118.00 tax-included** under an 18% included VAT, qty 1, no fiscal-position remap.

On screen, `_get_tax_totals_summary` back-computes:

- Base (untaxed): 118.00 / 1.18 = **100.00**
- Tax: **18.00**
- Total: **118.00** → `amount_total`

When the session closes, `_prepare_account_move_line_data` runs the engine over this order and groups its base lines; the resulting session invoice's own tax sync emits the tax line. The move posts (the cash side lands on the payment/receivable line; the sale + tax side looks like):

| Account | Debit | Credit | Tax grid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Income (sales) | | 100.00 | base grid via line tags |
| VAT collected (tax repartition account) | | 18.00 | tax grid (`tax_tag_ids`) |
| Cash / receivable (counterpart) | 118.00 | | |

The 100.00 base and 18.00 tax are exactly what an invoice for the same product would post, to the same accounts and grids. If ten customers buy this item in the session, the engine still computes each order's 100/18 (rounded per order), then the grouping function [pos_order.py:1206-1213](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L1206) collapses them into a single 1000.00 sales line, and the invoice's tax sync emits a single 180.00 tax line.

### Gotchas

- **Raw vs remapped taxes.** `tax_ids` is the product's taxes; `tax_ids_after_fiscal_position` ([pos_order_line.py:54](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L54)) is what actually computes and posts. When debugging wrong tax in POS, check the order's `fiscal_position_id`, not just the line's `tax_ids`.
- **Config drives the fiscal position, not the partner.** A new POS order takes `default_fiscal_position_id` ([pos_order.py:499](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L499)); the cashier can only switch among `fiscal_position_ids`. The partner's own fiscal position is not auto-applied the way it is on a sale order.
- **`iface_tax_included` is display-only.** It changes shown prices, not the tax math — inclusion is a property of the `account.tax` record.
- **One move per session, grouped — and in 20 it is an invoice.** Don't expect a per-order tax line. Base lines are aggregated by `(account_id, product_id?, tax_ids)` ([pos_order.py:1206-1213](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order.py#L1206)) and the tax lines come from the invoice's own sync; reconciling a single receipt against the session move means matching the grouped totals. Code that used to look for a `move_type='entry'` session move must now look for `out_invoice` / `out_refund` ([pos_session.py:981](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L981)).
- **Invoiced orders are excluded from the session aggregation** ([pos_session.py:1137-1143](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L1137)) — their taxes live on their own `account.move`, and the session will not close until those are posted ([pos_session.py:1145-1153](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_session.py#L1145)). Double-counting fears are unfounded, but the session move will not contain those amounts.
- **A missing income account fails the close.** `_prepare_base_lines_for_taxes_computation` raises a `UserError` naming the product when neither the product's income account nor the journal default resolves ([pos_order_line.py:305-309](../addons/point_of_sale/models/pos_order_line.py#L305)) — a misconfigured product or tax surfaces only at session close, not at the register.

## Special Cases: Non-Deductible Tax, Multi-Currency, EDI & Credit Notes

The tax engine in [account_tax.py](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py) is built around two ideas that make these three cases work. First, every base line is computed *twice* — once in the document's foreign currency and once in company currency — and the engine keeps a **raw** (unrounded) copy of every amount alongside the rounded one. Second, certain behaviours are layered on as a "special type" of base line, identified by `special_type`, that the main loop treats differently. Non-deductible tax, multi-currency rounding, and EDI export all build on these two foundations.

A base line is the engine's unit of work. It is prepared by `_prepare_base_line_for_taxes_computation`, which sets `special_type`, the conversion `rate`, the `is_refund` flag, and the line's `currency_id` — see the dict built at [account_tax.py:1618-1676](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1618), with `special_type` at [account_tax.py:1628](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1628), `rate` at [account_tax.py:1632](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1632), and `is_refund` at [account_tax.py:1644](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1644).

### Non-Deductible Tax

**Business meaning.** On a vendor bill, some input VAT is not fully recoverable — a classic example is VAT on a car or on entertainment expenses, where the law lets you reclaim only part of the tax. The non-recoverable slice is not an asset (a receivable from the tax authority); it is a cost. So it must be *moved out of the tax accounts and folded into the expense/asset* the bill is paying for.

**How it is configured.** The recoverable percentage lives on the product line, not on the tax. **Changed in 20:** the field was renamed from `deductible_amount` to **`deductible_percentage`**, and its scale changed from **0–100 (default 100)** to **0–1 (default 1.0)** — [account_move_line.py:480-487](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L480). Its label is now "Professional Expense Deductibility Percentage". A constraint at [account_move_line.py:1968-1974](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1968) restricts anything below 1 to purchase documents and forces the value into the 0–1 range. When a line's deductibility is below 1, the move materialises extra accounting lines with `display_type` of `non_deductible_product` / `non_deductible_product_total` (the tax counterpart is `non_deductible_tax`), declared at [account_move_line.py:398-400](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L398).

**Also new in 20:** `account.tax` gained a read-only `non_deductible_amount` ([account_tax.py:200](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L200), computed at [account_tax.py:663](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L663)). It is a *different* thing from the line field: it sums the `factor_percent` of every `tax` repartition line that is **excluded from tax closing**, i.e. the share of the tax the configuration itself treats as a cost. Use it to spot partly non-deductible taxes; use `deductible_percentage` for the per-line, mixed-use case.

**How the engine handles it.** Those extra lines are turned into base lines flagged `special_type='non_deductible'`. The conversion happens in `_prepare_non_deductible_base_lines_for_taxes_computation_from_base_lines` at [account_move.py:1801](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1801): it filters product lines whose `deductible_percentage != 1` ([account_move.py:1810](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1810)), computes the non-deductible slice as `1 - deductible_percentage` of the subtotal ([account_move.py:1820-1822](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1820)), and emits one negative non-deductible base line per product line plus one positive aggregate line ([account_move.py:1825-1845](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1825)). The single-line path `_prepare_non_deductible_base_line_for_taxes_computation` at [account_move.py:1780](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1780) forces `special_mode='total_excluded'` and `special_type='non_deductible'`.

In the per-line computation, the non-deductible flag also strips reverse-charge tax data back out of the included total — see [account_tax.py:1763-1770](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1763). The comment there, "Only python side for professional with reverse charge", is the first signal that this feature is Python-only.

The real subtraction happens **after** the main tax-group loop, in `_get_tax_totals_summary`. Once normal tax groups are built, the engine collects the taxed non-deductible lines ([account_tax.py:3002-3007](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3002)), re-aggregates them per tax group, and then *removes* their tax and base contribution from each group total at [account_tax.py:3017-3020](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3017):

```python
tax_group['tax_amount'] -= tax_values['tax_amount']
tax_group['tax_amount_currency'] -= tax_values['tax_amount_currency']
tax_group['base_amount'] -= tax_values['base_amount']
tax_group['base_amount_currency'] -= tax_values['base_amount_currency']
```

The removed amount is stashed separately as `non_deductible_tax_amount` / `non_deductible_tax_amount_currency` on the tax group ([account_tax.py:3014-3015](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3014)) so the UI can still show it. The explicit comment at [account_tax.py:3001](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3001) — "this part is not implemented in the JS-part of the tax total summary computation" — is the key gotcha: the JS engine ([account_tax.js](../addons/account/static/src/helpers/account_tax.js)), which drives the live form view, does *not* perform this subtraction. The non-deductible correction only appears once the move is computed server-side.

**Worked example — 50% deductible VAT on a 100 bill.** A vendor bill line of 100 with 20% VAT, `deductible_percentage = 0.5` (**that is 0.5, not 50, since 20**):

| Step | Amount | Account |
|---|---|---|
| Untaxed base | 100.00 | Expense |
| Full VAT 20% | 20.00 | VAT input (provisional) |
| Non-deductible slice = 50% of tax | 10.00 | moved out of VAT |
| VAT group total after subtraction | 10.00 | VAT input (recoverable) |
| Non-deductible 10.00 folded into | 110.00 | Expense / asset |

The main loop first records VAT of 20.00. The non-deductible post-pass then subtracts 10.00 from the VAT group, leaving 10.00 recoverable, and the matching 10.00 lands on the expense via the non-deductible product line. Net: you reclaim 10.00, you expense 110.00, total cash out stays 120.00.

### Multi-Currency Tax

Every base line is computed in **both** currencies simultaneously. `_add_tax_details_in_base_line` produces the foreign-currency totals directly from the tax computation and derives the company-currency totals by dividing by `rate` — [account_tax.py:1772-1776](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1772):

```python
'raw_total_excluded_currency': taxes_computation['total_excluded'],
'raw_total_excluded':          taxes_computation['total_excluded'] / rate if rate else 0.0,
```

So `*_currency` fields are the foreign (document) currency, and the bare fields are company currency. The same pair is produced for each tax: `raw_tax_amount_currency` vs `raw_tax_amount`, `raw_base_amount_currency` vs `raw_base_amount` ([account_tax.py:1784-1796](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1784)). The field semantics are documented in the `_add_tax_details_in_base_line` docstring at [account_tax.py:1723](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1723).

**Where rounding happens.** Rounding is per currency, and each currency rounds with its *own* precision. Under `round_per_line` the company-currency raw totals are rounded immediately at conversion time ([account_tax.py:1780-1790](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1780)). The general rounding step is `_round_base_lines_tax_details` at [account_tax.py:2204](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2204): it loops over both currencies and rounds the raw value into the non-raw field with that currency's rounding —

```python
for suffix, currency in (('_currency', base_line['currency_id']), ('', company.currency_id)):
    tax_details[total_excluded_field] = currency.round(tax_details[f'raw_{total_excluded_field}'])
```

Because the two currencies round independently, the company-currency tax total is not simply the foreign tax total times the rate — each is rounded on its own scale, then deltas are smoothed across lines.

**Display.** The company `display_invoice_tax_company_currency` boolean ([company.py:156](../addons/account/models/company.py#L156), default `True`) controls whether the tax totals widget also shows amounts in company currency. It only takes effect on customer documents where the document currency differs from the company currency and there are real tax groups — see the guard in `_compute_tax_totals` at [account_move.py:1930-1935](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1930). Non-invoice moves (e.g. journal entries) set `tax_totals = None` because all their lines need not share one currency ([account_move.py:1937-1939](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1937)).

### EDI & Credit Notes

**Refund repartition selection.** A tax can split its result differently on an invoice versus a refund, via two One2many sets: `invoice_repartition_line_ids` ([account_tax.py:160](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L160)) and `refund_repartition_line_ids` ([account_tax.py:168](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L168)). The constraint at [account_tax.py:511](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L511) forces the two sets to stay structurally parallel (same length, same factor sequence). The selection is driven end-to-end by the base line's `is_refund` flag. In `_add_accounting_data_to_base_line_tax_details`, the very first decision picks the field name — [account_tax.py:2412-2415](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2412):

```python
if is_refund:
    repartition_lines_field = 'refund_repartition_line_ids'
else:
    repartition_lines_field = 'invoice_repartition_line_ids'
```

That chosen field name is then used uniformly for both the base tags ([account_tax.py:2431-2432](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2431)) and the tax repartition amounts ([account_tax.py:2435-2440](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2435)). `is_refund` originates from the move type: a credit note (`out_refund` / `in_refund`) sets it true — see [account_move.py:7294](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L7294), passed into every base line at [account_move.py:1709](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1709). So a credit note routes its tax to the refund accounts/tags and its base to the refund base tags, without any branching in the caller.

**Why EDI must consume the RAW amounts.** UBL / Factur-X XML reports each invoice line individually and demands high precision (typically 6–8 decimals). The rounding doc at [account_tax.py:2205-2245](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2205) is explicit: `_round_base_lines_tax_details` copies every `raw_`-prefixed float into its rounded counterpart, and "if you need to report the price excluded amount for a single line, you need to use `raw_total_excluded_currency` / `raw_total_excluded` instead of `total_excluded_currency` / `total_excluded` because the latest are rounded." The reason is the round-globally model: per-line amounts are deliberately rounded *and a delta is then redistributed across lines* so the document total is correct, even though no single rounded line equals `rate × base`. The worked example at [account_tax.py:2222-2240](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2222) shows two 21.53 lines whose raw excluded is `17.79338843` each; the rounded `17.79 + 17.79 = 35.58`, but the group rounds to `35.59`, so a `0.01` delta is pushed onto line 1. If EDI exported the rounded per-line numbers, the reverse-computed tax per line would not reconcile against the line base, and the receiver's validator would reject it.

Accordingly, `account_edi_ubl_cii` reads raw fields for line-level facts: the allowance/charge (discount) block uses `raw_discount_amount` ([account_edi_ubl.py:652](../addons/account_edi_ubl_cii/models/account_edi_ubl.py#L652)) and `raw_gross_total_excluded` ([account_edi_ubl.py:569](../addons/account_edi_ubl_cii/models/account_edi_ubl.py#L569)), and the reported unit price uses `raw_gross_price_unit` ([account_edi_ubl.py:574](../addons/account_edi_ubl_cii/models/account_edi_ubl.py#L574)). The engine even exposes a context flag, `compute_all_use_raw_base_lines`, that swaps the repartition computation onto `raw_tax_amount_currency` / `raw_*` instead of the rounded values — see [account_tax.py:2477-2479](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2477).

### Gotchas

- **Non-deductible tax is Python-only.** The post-loop subtraction at [account_tax.py:3001-3020](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L3001) is not mirrored in `account_tax.js`. The live form preview will show the full tax until the move is recomputed server-side. Do not assume parity with the JS engine for this feature.
- **Changed in 20: the line field is `deductible_percentage`, on a 0–1 scale.** It was `deductible_amount` on a 0–100 scale in 19 ([account_move_line.py:480](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L480)). Any code, import or report carrying the old name or the old scale is off by a factor of 100. Non-deductibility still lives on the line, restricted to purchase documents — there is no single "non-deductible tax" you just attach.
- **`*_currency` means foreign, bare means company.** This is the opposite of what newcomers guess. `total_excluded_currency` is the document currency; `total_excluded` is company currency ([account_tax.py:1772-1776](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L1772)).
- **Each currency rounds independently.** Company-currency tax is not foreign tax × rate after rounding. Computing it yourself reintroduces the rounding errors the engine works to avoid ([account_tax.py:2222-2245](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2222)).
- **Never round again for EDI.** Always read `raw_*` for per-line EDI values; rounding them yourself breaks the global delta distribution and the receiver's line-vs-total validation ([account_tax.py:2205-2245](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L2205)).
- **`invoice_` and `refund_` repartition must stay parallel.** The constraint at [account_tax.py:511](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L511) enforces equal length and matching factor order; you cannot give a refund a structurally different split.
- **`display_invoice_tax_company_currency` only shows on customer multi-currency invoices.** Vendor bills, same-currency invoices, and journal entries never display the secondary-currency tax column regardless of the company flag ([account_move.py:1930-1935](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L1930)).

## Tax Report & Tax Closing Entry

This section explains how the tax tags you put on move lines become a periodic tax report, and how that report turns into a posted accounting entry that moves your net VAT to a single payable (or receivable) account and locks the period.

### The community / enterprise split (read this first)

The single most important fact, which corrects any earlier impression in this doc:

> **Community (`addons/account`) owns the grids and the lock date; everything else — including, as of 20, the closing accounts themselves — lives in *enterprise* (`account_reports`).** Without enterprise there is no tax report computation, no place to configure the closing accounts, and no automatic closing move: you would have to journal the VAT transfer by hand.

> **Changed in 20 — the closing accounts moved out of community.** `account.tax.group` no longer has `tax_payable_account_id`, `tax_receivable_account_id` or `advance_tax_payment_account_id`. They now live on the enterprise **`account.return.type`** ([account_return.py:162-196](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L162)) as `company_dependent`-backed computed fields with a `compute_sql` twin, so one return type carries a different account triplet per company. Everything below that used to say "the tax group's accounts" now means "the return type's accounts".

Concretely:

| Concern | Where it lives | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Tax grids (`tag_ids` on the repartition line) | community `account.tax.repartition.line` | [account_tax.py:5159](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5159) |
| `use_in_tax_closing` flag on the repartition line | community `account.tax.repartition.line` | [account_tax.py:5164](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5164) |
| `tax_lock_date` on the company | community `res.company` | [company.py:84](../addons/account/models/company.py#L84) |
| **Closing accounts (payable / receivable / advance)** | **enterprise** `account.return.type` (**moved here in 20**) | [account_return.py:162-196](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L162) |
| Tax report computation from grids (tags) | **enterprise** `account.report` (`tax_tags` engine) | [account_report.py:4392](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_report.py#L4392) |
| Generating + posting the closing entry | **enterprise** `account.return` | [account_return.py:1993](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1993) |
| Tax-report-specific aggregation logic | **enterprise** generic tax report | [account_generic_tax_report.py:285](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_generic_tax_report.py#L285) |

### How grids become a report

When you build a localized VAT report, each report line carries an expression. The common VAT engine is `tax_tags`: the expression's formula is just a tag label, and creating the expression auto-creates an `account.account.tag`. Posting an invoice stamps each tax (and base) move line with the tags coming from its tax repartition line. The report then simply sums the balances of every move line carrying that tag, applying the sign of the tag — that summed number is what shows in the grid cell. This is documented directly in the engine's own docstring at [account_report.py:4393-4400](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_report.py#L4393), and the SQL that joins `account_move_line` to `account_account_tag` and groups by tag is at [account_report.py:4411-4426](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_report.py#L4411).

So a "grid" is not a stored aggregate — it is a live `SUM(balance)` over the move lines wearing a given tag, scoped to the report's period and country. The output VAT lines, input VAT lines, and reclaimable/payable boxes a localization needs are all just different tags summed this way.

### From report to closing entry

The closing entry is produced when a tax `account.return` is validated. The flow:

1. `action_validate` runs the period's checks, then calls `_proceed_with_locking` — [account_return.py:1493](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1493), [account_return.py:1508](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1508).
2. For a tax return, `_proceed_with_locking` calls `_generate_tax_closing_entries` — [account_return.py:1538-1540](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1538).
3. `_generate_tax_closing_entries` builds the move values per company and **posts** them — [account_return.py:1993-2005](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1993). The guard that the closing accounts are configured is `_ensure_tax_closing_accounts_configured` ([account_return.py:2025-2045](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2025)); **changed in 20**, it checks the **return type's** `tax_payable_account_id` / `tax_receivable_account_id` and its `RedirectWarning` opens the **Tax Return Type** form, not the tax-group list. The move uses the company's tax closing journal and is dated `date_to` (period end) — [account_return.py:2013-2020](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2013).

The lines are computed in `_compute_tax_closing_entry` — [account_return.py:2048](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2048). Its SQL sums the balance of each tax line (`tax_line_id`) per tax / account, but **only for repartition lines where `use_in_tax_closing` is true** — [account_return.py:2081](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2081). That flag ([account_tax.py:5164](../addons/account/models/account_tax.py#L5164)) is what excludes, e.g., non-deductible portions from the cash settlement.

For every tax it then emits a balancing line that *zeroes out* the VAT account (debit if the VAT account held a credit, credit if it held a debit) — [account_return.py:2113-2120](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2113) — and accumulates the running `total` against the **return type's** account triplet `(advance, receivable, payable)`, resolved once per company by `_get_tax_closing_accounts` — [account_return.py:2224-2230](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2224), accumulated at [account_return.py:2123-2125](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2123). **Changed in 20:** the triplet no longer varies per tax group, so every tax in the return consolidates onto one counterpart pair unless a localization overrides `_get_tax_closing_accounts`.

### How the tax-group accounts are consumed

`_add_tax_group_closing_items` ([account_return.py:2243](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2243)) turns each accumulated `total` into the counterpart line. The decision is just the sign of the net:

- If `total < 0` (output VAT exceeds input VAT — you owe the authorities) → the counterpart hits **`tax_payable_account_id`** as a credit.
- If `total > 0` (input VAT exceeds output VAT — the authorities owe you) → it hits **`tax_receivable_account_id`** as a debit.

See the sign-based account/side selection in `_create_tax_payable_receivable_line` at [account_return.py:2232-2238](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2232). Any prepaid VAT sitting on **`advance_tax_payment_account_id`** is folded in first: its balance up to `date_to` is read and added to the net before the payable/receivable split, and that advance account is itself balanced to zero — [account_return.py:2268-2280](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2268), using the helper at [account_return.py:2248-2257](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2248) and the carry-over account list at [account_return.py:2240-2241](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2240). The three fields' help text now lives on the return type — payable = "counterpart … when in favor of the authorities", receivable = "when in favor of the company", advance = downpayments considered by the closing — [account_return.py:162-196](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L162).

### Worked example

Period: one VAT tax group, output VAT account balance **210 (credit)**, input VAT account balance **80 (debit)**, no advance payment. Standard sign convention: a credit balance is negative, a debit is positive.

The closing move first flattens the two VAT accounts, then posts the net to the group's payable account:

| Account | Debit | Credit | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output VAT (collected) | 210 | | Balancing line to zero the 210 credit — [account_return.py:2113-2120](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2113) |
| Input VAT (deductible) | | 80 | Balancing line to zero the 80 debit |
| **VAT Payable** (`tax_payable_account_id` on the return type) | | **130** | Net `total = (-210) + 80 = -130`; negative → credit the payable account — [account_return.py:2232-2238](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2232) |

The entry balances (210 = 80 + 130) and leaves 130 owed to the tax authority on the payable account. If input VAT had been 250 instead of 80, `total = +40`, and the 40 would land as a **debit on `tax_receivable_account_id`** — a VAT credit you can reclaim or carry forward.

### Lock date interaction

Posting the closing move is paired with locking the period so the reported numbers can't drift. After the move is posted, `_proceed_with_locking` clears any active tax lock exceptions ([account_return.py:1542-1546](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1542)) and sets `company.tax_lock_date = self.date_to` ([account_return.py:1549-1556](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1549)) — but only when the report's country matches the main company's fiscal country and the new date actually moves the lock forward. The company field itself ([company.py:85](../addons/account/models/company.py#L85)) documents this: "The tax lock date is automatically set when the tax closing entry is posted." Any later entry dated on or before that date gets postponed per its journal sequence, so a filed period stays frozen.

### Gotchas

- **No enterprise → no engine, and since 20 no accounts either.** Community gives you the grids and the lock date field, but neither the closing accounts, nor the tax report figures, nor the closing move. Don't expect a closing entry from `account` alone.
- **`use_in_tax_closing` controls cash, not the report.** A repartition line can feed the report grid via its tag yet be excluded from the settlement move if `use_in_tax_closing` is off ([account_return.py:2081](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2081)). Report total and closing-entry total can legitimately differ.
- **Missing closing accounts hard-stop the close — but on the return type now.** If the `account.return.type` lacks a payable *or* receivable account for the company, `_ensure_tax_closing_accounts_configured` raises a `RedirectWarning` onto the Tax Return Type form and posts nothing — [account_return.py:2025-2045](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2025). Code or checklists that still point users at the tax-group list are stale.
- **Sign convention is balance-based, not "output vs input" by name.** The code keys off the sign of the net balance ([account_return.py:2232-2238](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2232)); it does not label accounts as input/output. A reversed expectation about which side is "payable" comes from misreading the sign.
- **Everything consolidates onto one triplet.** In 20 the `(advance, receivable, payable)` key comes from the return type, not per tax group ([account_return.py:2224-2230](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2224)), so all of a return's taxes normally merge into a single counterpart line. A localization wanting several counterparts must override `_get_tax_closing_accounts`.
- **The move is dated period-end and auto-posted.** It uses `date_to` and the dedicated closing journal (`company._get_tax_closing_journal()`), then `action_post()` immediately — there is no draft step you can review before posting in this path ([account_return.py:2013-2020](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2013), [account_return.py:2005](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2005)). The post runs with `account_bypass_tax_closing_lock_check` when the lock date was already moved to `date_to` ([account_return.py:2002-2005](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L2002)).

## Tax Lock Dates

### What it is and why it exists

A **Tax Lock Date** (`tax_lock_date`) is a company-level cut-off date. Once a tax return is filed for a period, the figures behind it must never silently change. The tax lock date enforces this: any *tax-relevant* journal item dated on or before it can no longer be added, edited, or removed on a posted entry. This protects already-submitted tax statements (VAT returns, etc.) from being invalidated by back-dated edits.

The field lives on the company and is `tracking=True`, so every change is logged on the company chatter. Its help text states it is "automatically set when the tax closing entry is posted" — see [company.py:85](../addons/account/models/company.py#L85).

The key nuance versus the other lock dates: the tax lock only bites on lines that **affect the tax report**. A purely financial reclassification dated before the tax lock is allowed; touching a line that carries taxes or tax tags is not.

### The family of lock dates

The tax lock is one of several. Odoo groups them in [company.py:60-72](../addons/account/models/company.py#L60):

| Field | String | Scope | Reversible? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `fiscalyear_lock_date` | Global Lock Date | All entries | Yes (exception) |
| `tax_lock_date` | Tax Return Lock Date | Entries that affect the tax report | Yes (exception) |
| `sale_lock_date` | Sales Lock Date | Sale-journal entries | Yes (exception) |
| `purchase_lock_date` | Purchase Lock date | Purchase-journal entries | Yes (exception) |
| `hard_lock_date` | Hard Lock Date | All entries — irreversible | **No** |

The first four are the `SOFT_LOCK_DATE_FIELDS` ([company.py:60](../addons/account/models/company.py#L60)); adding `hard_lock_date` gives `LOCK_DATE_FIELDS` ([company.py:67](../addons/account/models/company.py#L67)). "Soft" means an audited exception can be granted; the hard lock admits none.

### user_tax_lock_date: per-user, exception-aware resolution

The raw `tax_lock_date` is the company policy. What actually applies to *the current user right now* is the computed `user_tax_lock_date` ([company.py:112](../addons/account/models/company.py#L112)). It is computed by `_compute_user_tax_lock_date` ([company.py:552](../addons/account/models/company.py#L552)), which delegates to the generic `_get_user_lock_date('tax_lock_date', ...)` at [company.py:749](../addons/account/models/company.py#L749).

`_get_user_lock_date` does two things:

1. **Walks parent companies.** It iterates `self.sudo().parent_ids` and takes the strictest (max) lock across the company branch — a branch inherits its parent's lock.
2. **Applies the active exception for this user**, unless called with `ignore_exceptions=True`. It searches `account.lock_exception` for an active record matching either this user or everyone (`user_id = None`), whose relaxed date is earlier than the company date, and uses that looser date instead ([company.py:765-780](../addons/account/models/company.py#L765)).

The compute is decorated `@api.depends_context('uid', 'ignore_exceptions')` ([company.py:551](../addons/account/models/company.py#L551)), so the same company yields different effective lock dates per user and per context. Toggling `ignore_exceptions` in the context is how callers compare "the policy" against "what this user may actually do" — that is exactly how `_get_violated_soft_lock_date` decides whether a date is genuinely blocked ([company.py:798](../addons/account/models/company.py#L798)): if the date is within the strict lock but outside the user's exception-relaxed lock, no violation is reported.

For the fiscal side there is also `_get_user_fiscal_lock_date(journal, ...)` ([company.py:784](../addons/account/models/company.py#L784)), which `max`-combines the fiscal-year, hard, and (per journal type) sale/purchase user locks. The tax lock is deliberately *not* in that mix — it is checked separately so that non-tax edits in a closed fiscal-but-not-tax period behave correctly.

### The constraint that fires: `_check_tax_lock_date`

The enforcement point is `_check_tax_lock_date` on the move line ([account_move_line.py:1882](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1882)). For each line it:

1. Skips anything whose move is not `posted` (drafts are free to edit).
2. Asks the company for tax + hard lock violations only — `fiscalyear=False, sale=False, purchase=False, tax=True, hard=True` via `_get_lock_date_violations` ([account_move_line.py:1887-1894](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1887), defined at [company.py:817](../addons/account/models/company.py#L817)).
3. Raises only if there is a violation **and** the line actually affects the tax report, i.e. `line._affect_tax_report()` is truthy. That helper returns true when the line has `tax_ids`, a `tax_line_id`, or tax-applicable `tax_tag_ids` ([account_move_line.py:1878-1880](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1878)).

The exact error raised at [account_move_line.py:1896-1898](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1896):

> The operation is refused as it would impact an already issued tax statement. Please change the journal entry date or the following lock dates to proceed: …

The list of dates is formatted by `_format_lock_dates` ([company.py:855](../addons/account/models/company.py#L855)), e.g. `Tax Return Lock Date (12/31/2025)`.

This check is wired into `account.move.write` at several points ([account_move.py:4043](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4043), [account_move.py:4048](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4048), [account_move.py:4093](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4093)): when a posted move's date or name changes, when it is un-posted, and after writing a `date`/`state`. It also fires from `account.move.line` create/write/unlink ([account_move_line.py:2165](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L2165), [account_move_line.py:2255](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L2255), [account_move_line.py:2347](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L2347)). So both editing into a locked period and editing a move already in one are caught.

### Draft-time warning (no block, just a heads-up)

While a move is still draft, Odoo does not block — it warns. `_compute_tax_lock_date_message` ([account_move.py:2019](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L2019)) builds `tax_lock_date_message` ([account_move.py:780](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L780)) from `_get_lock_date_message` ([account_move.py:7475](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L7475)). If the chosen date falls in a locked period, the message tells the user the entry will instead be **accounted on a later date upon posting** (the next open date from `_get_accounting_date`). This surfaces as a form alert at [account_move.py:2601-2605](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L2601). The underlying violations come from `_get_violated_lock_dates`, which routes through the company method passing the move's journal so sale/purchase locks are considered too ([account_move.py:7466](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L7466), [company.py:865](../addons/account/models/company.py#L865)).

### account.lock_exception: audited, temporary relief

A soft lock can be relaxed for a specific user (or everyone) for a limited time via `account.lock_exception` ([account_lock_exception.py:11](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L11)). Key design points:

- **One exception changes exactly one lock field.** `create` parses a convenience val like `{'tax_lock_date': '2025-01-01'}` into `lock_date_field='tax_lock_date'` + `lock_date`, and rejects vals touching more than one field ([account_lock_exception.py:176](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L176)).
- **Scope.** `user_id` defaults to the current user; leaving it empty makes the exception apply to *everyone* ([account_lock_exception.py:37](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L37)). `end_datetime` empty means "valid forever" ([account_lock_exception.py:46](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L46)).
- **State** is computed: `revoked` if inactive, `expired` if past `end_datetime`, else `active` ([account_lock_exception.py:104](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L104)).
- **Audit trail.** Creation posts a tracking message to the company chatter recording who got the exception, until when, and why ([account_lock_exception.py:188](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L188)). It cannot be duplicated ([account_lock_exception.py:213](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L213)). Revoking requires the *Adviser* (`group_account_manager`) role ([account_lock_exception.py:231](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L231)). After the fact, `action_show_audit_trail_during_exception` ([account_lock_exception.py:271](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L271)) lists every journal item changed while the exception was open, so the relaxation is fully reviewable.
- **Cache coherence.** Creating or revoking an exception invalidates the affected `user_*_lock_date` computed fields ([account_lock_exception.py:158](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L158)), so the new effective lock takes effect immediately.

### Relationship to the tax closing entry

Closing a tax period is what normally *sets* the lock. In enterprise `account_reports`, when a tax return is processed, `tax_lock_date` is advanced to the period's `date_to` for each company ([account_return.py:1555](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1555)), but only if the report country matches the fiscal country and the new date is later than the current lock ([account_return.py:1551-1552](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1551)). Crucially, the same routine first **revokes any active tax-lock exceptions** for those companies ([account_return.py:1542-1547](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1542)) — closing the books means no lingering relaxation survives into the newly filed period.

> **Changed in 20 — resetting a closing no longer rolls the lock back.** In 19, `action_reset_tax_return` pushed `tax_lock_date` back to the day before the period start. In 20 the lock is simply left where it is; instead the reset **refuses** when deleting the period's carryover values would touch a tax-locked period, raising *"You cannot reset this closing entry to draft…"* ([account_return.py:1741-1748](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1741)). Rolling the lock back is now a deliberate manual act (or a lock exception).

### Worked example

Company tax lock date = **2025-12-31**. The 2025 VAT return is filed.

A user opens a *posted* customer invoice **INV/2025/0042** dated **2025-11-20** carrying 18% VAT, and tries to change its date to 2025-11-25:

1. `account.move.write` sees `date` changing on a posted move and calls `move.line_ids._check_tax_lock_date()` ([account_move.py:4043](../addons/account/models/account_move.py#L4043)).
2. For the VAT line, `move.date` (2025-11-20) ≤ `user_tax_lock_date` (2025-12-31), so `_get_lock_date_violations` returns `[(date(2025,12,31), 'tax_lock_date')]`.
3. The line has `tax_ids`, so `_affect_tax_report()` is truthy ([account_move_line.py:1880](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1880)).
4. The constraint raises:

> The operation is refused as it would impact an already issued tax statement. Please change the journal entry date or the following lock dates to proceed: **Tax Return Lock Date (12/31/2025)**.

To proceed legitimately, an Adviser creates an `account.lock_exception` with `{'tax_lock_date': '2025-11-19'}` for that user. `user_tax_lock_date` recomputes to 2025-11-19, the invoice's date (2025-11-20) is now *after* it, no violation fires, and the edit goes through — fully logged on the company chatter and visible in the exception's audit trail.

### Gotchas

- **Tax lock ≠ fiscal lock.** A line with no taxes/tax tags is *never* blocked by the tax lock, even when dated before it. The check only fires on lines where `_affect_tax_report()` is truthy ([account_move_line.py:1878-1880](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1878)).
- **Draft moves are not blocked, only warned.** The block lives in a constraint that early-returns unless `move.state == 'posted'` ([account_move_line.py:1885-1886](../addons/account/models/account_move_line.py#L1885)). Drafts just get a banner saying posting will shift the accounting date forward.
- **The effective date is `user_tax_lock_date`, not `tax_lock_date`.** An active exception (or a parent company's stricter lock) changes what actually applies. Always reason about the `user_` field, resolved per user and per `ignore_exceptions` context.
- **Closing a tax period silently revokes tax-lock exceptions** ([account_return.py:1542-1547](../enterprise/account_reports/models/account_return.py#L1542)). Relief granted before filing does not carry over — and, **changed in 20**, resetting the return does not put the lock date back either.
- **Hard lock has no escape.** It is checked alongside the tax lock (`hard=True`) but no `account.lock_exception` can relax it — the exception model only offers the four soft fields ([account_lock_exception.py:51](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L51)).
- **Exceptions are per single field.** You cannot loosen tax and fiscal locks with one record; `create` raises if more than one lock field is supplied ([account_lock_exception.py:177](../addons/account/models/account_lock_exception.py#L177)).
- **Branch inheritance.** `_get_user_lock_date` takes the max over `parent_ids` ([company.py:759-782](../addons/account/models/company.py#L759)); a child company can be more locked than its own `tax_lock_date` suggests because the parent's lock is folded in.
